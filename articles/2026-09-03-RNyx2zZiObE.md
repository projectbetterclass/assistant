**The claim.** AI chips are being financed like airplanes. A special-purpose company borrowed $35B to buy Google TPU racks and rent them to Anthropic, so the debt isn't on Anthropic's books before its stock-market listing. The deal only worked because Broadcom promised what the chips will be worth later. That promise is now worrying Broadcom's own lenders.

## How the deal is built
| Layer | Size | Interest | Who's really at risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senior notes (bank) | $6B | – | Backed by Broadcom's promise |
| Senior notes | $24B | 5.75% | Backed by Broadcom's promise |
| Junior notes | $4.5B | 8.5% | No backstop, paid last |
| Equity (Apollo's Atlas SP) | $0.8B | – | Owns the vehicle |

- Same chips, same renter, same day, yet the unprotected lenders charged ~3 points more. Senior lenders are really betting on Broadcom, not Anthropic.
- If Anthropic stops paying, the chips are sold or Broadcom takes over the lease. Broadcom's exposure is capped at **$29B**.
- It's a platform: Broadcom's AI financing vehicle aims at 20+ GW through 2028, for Anthropic and OpenAI. Bank of America models up to ~$370B of senior debt by 2029. A next deal of $60B+ (maybe $100B with junior money) is in talks.
- NVIDIA's version is a target: $500B+ from six firms (Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman, KKR), with value support of up to 25%, case by case.
- Why lenders like it: calling AI compute "infrastructure" with contracted rent brings in pension and insurance money, the money that buys toll roads.

## Four problems underneath
1. **Hidden debt:** US accounting only books these promises when a loss is likely, so they sit in footnotes. JPMorgan calls it "phantom leverage", and Bloomberg counts ~$70B at the big AI firms. Meta's off-balance-sheet promises are near $628B.
2. **Used chips:** the backstop only costs Broadcom if Anthropic stops paying *and* the chips are worth less than the debt. There's almost no market for used AI chips, unlike 20-year-old jets.
3. **Conflicts:** Broadcom co-designs the chips with Google, Google owns part of Anthropic, and Morgan Stanley advised and sold the debt.
4. **Rivals:** Google took an option on $12.2B of Marvell shares, which grows with every chip it buys from Marvell.

Markets already reacted. The cost of insuring Broadcom's debt jumped more than Oracle's. Bank of America downgraded its bonds, and Moody's and S&P count the promise like debt. The stock was ~25% below its June high despite +48% revenue. The last minutes are an ad for his agency and courses.

**How the call aged.** No buy call. Since Sep 3: Broadcom +1.2%, Apollo −11.8%, Blackstone −17.0% (the private-credit lenders fell hard), Marvell +31.8%, S&P 500 +0.8%.

**Aswath check.** These guarantees are exactly the "all debt" step in L13. Aswath counts leases and similar promises as debt, and S&P does the same here. Add an expected cost for the $29B backstop before you value Broadcom's equity. It also changes Broadcom's cost of debt and its debt weight (L06, L07).
