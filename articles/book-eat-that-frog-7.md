**The argument.** Technology becomes the enemy when you feel you have to be reachable all the time: every notification pulls you off your frog, and starting the day in your inbox makes you chase that feeling all day. Turn things off, use your devices to protect what matters, and give the important work long, unbroken stretches.

## Technology is a terrible master
- **The addiction.** People check their phones about 46 to 85 times a day, twice as often as they think. At one business lunch, half of Tracy's table kept their heads bowed after grace: they were on their phones.
- **The email client.** A client buried in email applied the 80/20 rule: deleted and unsubscribed from the 80% of little value; of the rest, only 4% needed an answer straight away, and the other 16% went into an action folder.
- **Take back your time.** An auto-reply that you check email twice a day. A journalist came back from holiday to 700 emails and deleted them all: someone sending you an email doesn't own a piece of your life. If news is really important, someone will tell you.
- **Zones of silence.** Turn off computer and phone for an hour each morning and afternoon, and unplug for one full day a week.

## Technology is a wonderful servant
- **Use it to protect what matters.** Close every program you don't need, block distracting sites, and turn off all notifications.
- **Emergencies.** Keep one channel only family carers or key people know about, and leave just that open.
- **Your calendar.** Don't click accept on every invitation, and book big blocks for important tasks like appointments.
- **Make it work for you.** Use a digital to-do list to delegate and to remind you of your frogs. Post a big goal and daily progress publicly, so slacking shows.

## Focus your attention
- **The dopamine hit.** Each new message gives a small burst of dopamine, like a slot machine's bell. Start the day with a few, and it's hard to focus for the rest of it.
- **Multitasking is task shifting.** After an interruption it takes about 17 minutes to get your full attention back.
- **The fixes.** Don't check email first thing. Turn off sounds. Check twice a day, at 11 am and 3 pm. Give people in meetings your full attention.
- **Double your productivity.** Start on your most important task first thing, work 90 minutes without distraction, take 15 minutes off, then another 90 minutes. Only then check email.

**The idea worth keeping.** Devices off, notifications off, and two 90-minute blocks on your frog before you check anything.

**Try it on your work.** Tomorrow morning, don't check messages until you've done one 90-minute block on your AI service's frog, with notifications off. Afterwards, note how far you got.
