**The claim.** Wedbush analyst Dan Ives says Palantir is "involved" in 70–80% of all AI use cases and will be worth $1T within two or three years, roughly double today's $350–450B. Carbutt has covered Palantir since 2022 and finds the argument less mad the longer he looks.

## Ives' case
- Don't judge it as a software stock on a crazy multiple. It's a share of a $3–4T AI build-out, where ~30–40% goes to software and Palantir takes a big slice. It's not winner-takes-all.
- **US commercial revenue +149%** to $764M a quarter, up ~380% in two years. It's ~45% of sales but 80–90% of the valuation.
- **US government +90%** to $809M. AI projects jump the queue even in a shutdown.
- **Sales efficiency:** about 70 salespeople, and Karp says only ~7 really sell. A normal company that size would need thousands.
- Customers spend 57% more than a year ago (net dollar retention 157%). 220 deals over $1M in one quarter.
- **Cash:** revenue $1.9B (+93%), free cash flow $1.22B for the quarter at a 63% margin, $3.4B over 12 months, $9.2B of cash and no debt. Ives expects $7–8B of free cash flow in a few years.
- What "70–80% involved" means: Palantir is the live map of a company's data that any AI model plugs into. Being involved isn't the same as getting paid, and the number comes from Ives' own team's checks.

## The pushback (CNBC's Mike Santoli)
| | |
|---|---|
| Free cash flow yield on Ives' future $8B | ~1.6% |
| Microsoft's yield today, on cash it already makes | about the same |
| P/E | ~140–160 |

Palantir and Robinhood have traded almost in step for two years, so part of the price is excitement, not the business. Ives' answer is "think long term, like early Amazon." That's fair, and it's also what was said about many stocks that never grew into their prices.

**Risks Ives names.** Politics: local votes against data centers and regulation before the midterms. Investor patience on free cash flow across AI, especially in 2027. A build-out that only settles in 2028–29. And AI models eating software, which is the risk to the whole thesis. The last minutes are an ad for Carbutt's agency and his Palantir course.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 8: Palantir +22.8% (about $209 a share), Microsoft +8.3%, Robinhood −7.1%, S&P 500 +1.8%. Halfway to the trillion in one month, on price alone.

**Aswath check.** Santoli did the right math. Turn the price into the cash flows it requires (L22). At about half a trillion, a 1.6% yield on cash flows years away means you are paying for growth after that too. Aswath's test: how fast must free cash flow grow for 10 years to justify this, and is that "possible, plausible, probable" (L02, L20)?
