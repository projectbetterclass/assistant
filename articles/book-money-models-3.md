**The argument.** Three more ways to get strangers in the door with something free or cheap: a stripped-down decoy next to a far better premium option, more free stuff than paid stuff in one purchase, and a choice between paying full price later if it works or less now with bonuses. The point of every attraction offer is the same: enough cash up front to cover the cost of the customer.

## The decoy offer
- **The story: the $5 tanning pass.** A mentor's salons sold a five-day pass. Customers wanted to get darker than five days allows, and rushing burns, so: "a turkey takes three hours; double the heat and you burn it." Then: credit the pass toward a $19.99 monthly membership. Years later, when Hormozi's gym leads got too expensive, a free basic option next to a $399 premium one had 70 to 80% choosing the premium.
- **How it works.** Advertise a free or discounted lesser version. When leads come in, present the premium version beside it: more features, personal help, a guarantee. Either way you close them, and a decoy customer can be upgraded later.
- **Make the contrast huge.** Strip the decoy down (fewer parts, older, less personal, no guarantee) and make the premium as good as possible. Advertise the benefit ("a 21-day transformation"), not the features.
- **Free or discount.** Free brings more leads; a discount brings fewer, but more of them show up. Low show-up rates (doctors, lawyers, dentists) call for a discount.
- **Premium first.** Ask "Are you here for free stuff, or lasting results?" Most say results: show the premium. Otherwise show both and ask which gets them to their goal faster. Sell it with assumed, almost bored confidence.
- **Know their results better than they do.** That's what makes the premium offer the clear choice.

## Buy X, get Y free
- **The story: the boot shop.** A Nashville store's sign said "buy one pair, get two free". One pair was $600, three times a normal price, and bachelorette parties bought matching sets all day.
- **How it works.** The same price as three, framed as one plus two free. 18 months of service sells better as "buy 6 months, get 12 free" than "buy 12, get 6 free".
- **The rules.** Raise prices for real before giving things away. Give more free than paid. The free things can be different (one shirt, three pairs of socks), and "buy one get two free" usually beats "33% off".
- **Manage the cash.** A year of payments up front must still pay for a year of delivery. For fast cash, offer it to up to 10% of existing customers, and keep offering prepaid customers more: they spend the most.

## Pay less now, or pay more later
- **The story: double your reading speed.** A reading course offered $0 today and $297 tomorrow, cancelled if your speed didn't double (attendance required), or $97 now with the recordings. Hormozi's speed doubled in two hours.
- **How it works.** Pay later is effectively free: no risk, a conditional guarantee, and you get their card on file. Once they accept that, offer pay now at 20 to 50% off with bonuses.
- **Promise a clear yes or no.** Shoulder pain rated 1 to 10 before and after. Make the guarantee's conditions the things that get people the most value, like showing up.
- **Tune it.** Too many choose pay later: discount pay now more or add bonuses. Over 10% cancel: you promised too much, the conditions are too loose, or the price is too high.
- **Hint at what's next.** "Everyone wants to know about the next programme; I'll get to it at the end." People buy more when they've been waiting for it.

## Why attraction offers matter
- They turned $1,000 into $10 million in 10 months for Hormozi, because every time one returned money, it got doubled down on.
- With customers coming in, the next job is to boost 30-day profit by selling them more: upsells.

**The idea worth keeping.** Know what gets customers results better than they do, then put the cheap thing next to the thing that works, and let them pick.

**Try it on your work.** For your AI service, write a decoy and a premium option side by side: what the free or cheap version leaves out, what the premium adds, and the guarantee.
