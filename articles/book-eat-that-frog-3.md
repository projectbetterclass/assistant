**The argument.** A task is important because of what happens if you do it, or don't. Thinking long-term about today's choices is what separates people who get ahead. You'll always put things off; the skill is choosing to put off the things that don't matter, and sorting the rest by consequence.

## Consider the consequences
- **Long time perspective.** Harvard sociologist Edward Banfield found that the best predictor of getting ahead in life was thinking years ahead about the choices you make today. What you intend for the future shapes what you do now.
- **Short-term pleasure, long-term cost.** The most important tasks often have big long-term consequences and little reward today: learning a skill, the hard proposal, the first call.
- **Three questions.** What are my highest-value activities? What can I, and only I, do that, if done well, would make a real difference? What is the most valuable use of my time right now?
- **Forced efficiency.** There's never enough time to do everything, but there's always enough time for the most important thing.

## Practice creative procrastination
- **Everyone procrastinates.** The difference is what you put off: put off the low-value tasks, on purpose.
- **Posteriorities.** Just as you set priorities, decide what to stop doing. You can only take on something new by dropping something old.
- **Zero-based thinking.** Ask of each activity: knowing what I now know, would I start doing this again today? If not, stop as soon as you can.

## The ABCDE method
- **A: must do.** Serious consequences if you don't. Number them: A-1, A-2, A-3. A-1 is your frog.
- **B: should do.** Mild consequences. Never do a B while an A is left undone.
- **C: nice to do.** No consequences either way: coffee, chats, most email.
- **D: delegate.** Anything someone else could do, so you have time for your As.
- **E: eliminate.** Things you do from habit that no longer matter.

**The idea worth keeping.** A task matters by its consequences: sort the list A to E, start on A-1, and let the low-value things wait on purpose.

**Try it on your work.** Mark tomorrow's list A to E. Write down one E you'll stop doing for good, and start the day on A-1.
