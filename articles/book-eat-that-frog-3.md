**The argument.** A task is important because of what happens if you do it, or don't. Thinking long-term about today's choices is what separates people who get ahead. You'll always put something off; the skill is choosing to put off the things that don't matter, and sorting the rest by consequence.

## Consider the consequences
- **Long time perspective.** After more than 50 years of research, Edward Banfield of Harvard concluded that thinking long-term is the best single predictor of moving up in society, more than family, education or intelligence. Successful people think 5, 10 and 20 years ahead.
- **Future intent shapes present actions.** Something important has long-term consequences; something unimportant has few or none. Before starting anything, ask what happens if you do it, or don't.
- **Delay gratification.** Coming in earlier, reading in your field and taking courses pay off later; coffee and chatting feel good now.
- **Forced efficiency.** There's never enough time to do everything, but there's always enough for the most important thing.
- **Deadlines are an excuse.** Working under last-minute pressure means more stress, more mistakes and more redoing. Add 20% to every estimate as a buffer.
- **Three questions.** What are my highest-value activities? What can I, and only I, do that, if done well, will make a real difference (Peter Drucker's question)? What is the most valuable use of my time right now?

## Practice creative procrastination
- **Everyone procrastinates.** High and low performers differ in what they put off. Put off the small, low-value tasks on purpose.
- **Posteriorities.** A priority is something you do more of, and sooner; a posteriority is something you do less of, and later, if at all. To start something new, stop something old.
- **Say no.** Warren Buffett: "I just say no to everything that is not absolutely vital to me at the moment." A friend of Tracy's cut golf from three or four rounds a week to one, and got their home life back.
- **Zero-based thinking.** Knowing what I now know, would I start doing this again today? If not, stop.

## The ABCDE method
- **A: must do.** Serious consequences either way, like a key customer visit. Number them A-1, A-2; A-1 is your biggest, ugliest frog.
- **B: should do.** Mild consequences, like an unimportant call back. Never do a B while an A is undone: never chase a tadpole while a frog is waiting.
- **C: nice to do.** No consequences: coffee with a colleague, personal errands.
- **D: delegate.** Anything someone else can do, to free time for the As only you can do.
- **E: eliminate.** Things that once mattered but don't now, often done from habit.
- **Then start A-1.** Stay on it until it's finished. Practise this on every list for a month, and it becomes a habit.

**The idea worth keeping.** A task matters by its consequences: sort the list A to E, start on A-1, and let the low-value things wait on purpose.

**Try it on your work.** Mark tomorrow's list A to E. Write down one thing you'll stop doing for good, and start the day on A-1.
