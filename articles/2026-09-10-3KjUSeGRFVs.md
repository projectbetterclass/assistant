**The claim.** NVIDIA is more than a chip seller now. It returns huge amounts of cash, sells into every AI model including the cheap open ones, and invests in and guarantees the customers who buy its chips. That last part brings new upside, and a new way to lose: if a customer can't pay, NVIDIA can be hit twice.

## The quarter
- Revenue $96.2B, more than double a year ago. Data centers were $89B of it. Net income was $59.7B at a 75% gross margin.
- Customers are broadening: $49B came from the biggest cloud firms and $40B from newer clouds, industry and enterprises. The second group grew faster, +25% vs +13% from the quarter before.
- Next quarter's guidance is $108B, and roughly 70% growth is expected the year after, limited by supply, not demand.
- NVIDIA's own supply commitments jumped from $119B to $279B. It's paying ahead for demand it expects.
- It returned about $26B in buybacks and dividends. The dividend went from 1 cent to 25 cents a quarter in May. The path he sees is Apple's: a cash machine for decades.

## The three things underneath
1. **Cash.** Buybacks only help if the price paid is sensible.
2. **Cheaper AI.** When DeepSeek made models cheaper (Jan 2025), NVIDIA lost ~$600B in a day. But cheaper answers make new uses worth it, and agents need many model calls per task. Amazon alone will add 2 million NVIDIA chips through 2029.
3. **Backing customers.** NVIDIA guarantees up to $105B of OpenAI's lease on an Ohio campus from 2028. It's building $500B+ financing platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman and KKR, and may support up to 25% of a deal. It also holds stakes in SpaceX (~$21B) and Intel (~$30B), about 80% of its listed portfolio.

**Risks he names.** Margins dip to 71–72% next quarter because memory got more expensive. China is assumed at zero, which leaves room for Huawei. "Circular" demand: NVIDIA backs a customer who then buys NVIDIA. The portfolio is concentrated in two names. And if demand disappoints, chip sales and the backed customers suffer at the same time. The last few minutes are an ad for his YouTube agency and courses.

**How the call aged.** No buy call, just a shareholder's checklist. Since Sep 10, NVIDIA is up 5.0% against 2.7% for the S&P 500.

**Aswath check.** This is the "loose ends" step (L13). A valuation of NVIDIA's operations should add the SpaceX and Intel stakes at market value. It should also treat the Ohio guarantee as a possible liability, not ignore it. Then the margin question: is 75% the normal level, or a peak to normalize (L08)?
