**The argument.** Money you have attracts people who want some of it, and misfortunes nobody can predict. Rodan learns from the gold lender Mathon how to judge a loan and protect the principal; the old soldier Banzar watches Babylon's walls hold off an army. Both say the same thing: protect what you have before you need to.

## The gold lender
- **Rodan's problem.** Rodan, a spear maker, is given fifty pieces of gold by the king. Rodan's sister wants it lent to Rodan's brother-in-law, to start out as a merchant, and Rodan doesn't know how to refuse. Rodan asks Mathon the gold lender for advice.
- **The chest of tokens.** Mathon keeps a token from every borrower, and tells the story behind each. The safest loans are to borrowers whose property is worth more than the loan, or who earn enough to repay it. Loans backed by neither, given out of sympathy, are the ones that bring heartache.
- **Lending to people without experience.** A loan to start a trade someone doesn't know is likely to be lost, and the friendship with it. Better to help them learn the trade first.
- **The motto.** "Better a little caution than a great regret."
- **Rodan's choice.** Mathon advises putting the gold where it's safe and earns steadily, and lending only where repayment is sure.

## The walls of Babylon
- **The siege.** While Babylon's army is away, an Assyrian army attacks the city. Old Banzar, guarding a gate, hears the fighting and reassures the frightened people: the great walls hold, and the city is saved.
- **The lesson.** We all need walls: savings, insurance and safe investments that protect us and our families from the misfortunes we can't see coming.

**The idea worth keeping.** Protect the principal: lend only where repayment is secure, and build your walls before the attack comes.

**Try it on your work.** Check your FIRE plan's emergency buffer: how many months of fixed costs would it cover? If it's less than you'd want, add a monthly step toward it.
