**The idea.** The richest he ever felt wasn't after the big exits. It was the first $100,000 in the bank, because it ended the monthly worry about rent and food. His six steps: cut costs, protect your time, pick a skill people already pay for, learn it by doing, spend on the right things, and don't raise your lifestyle.

## The six steps
1. **Cut costs so you can take risks.** No eating out, no new clothes, the cheapest housing you can find, a paid-off car. He split a bedroom for $300–400 a month and stayed there while earning about $20,000 a month.
2. **Save time.** A 9-to-5 still leaves 5–9 am and 5–9 pm. Without a job: four hours promoting, four delivering, four building. Keep "maker" time (no meetings) apart from "manager" time, because switching kills output. His best habit: the first four to six hours of the day to himself.
3. **Pick a skill people already pay for.** For businesses: content, outreach, funnels; each could be a business on its own. For consumers: read your own bank statement. His 1-1-1 rule: one product, one kind of customer, one channel until $1M.
4. **Learning means the same situation and a new behaviour.** Think 10,000 tries, not 10,000 hours. Do lots of reps, study your best 10%, find what they have that the rest don't, and test the differences one at a time. The fastest way is to pay someone good for one-on-one help.
5. **Spend on three things:** tools, help putting it into practice (courses, tutoring), and attempts (ads, editing software).
6. **Don't raise your lifestyle.** $100K in the bank, not in revenue. Be rich, don't look rich.

**Watch for.** There's an ad for his book bundle in the middle. His "cut everything" is deliberately extreme; the principle is the gap between what you earn and what you spend.

**Try it this week.** You have a note called "cost breakdown for fire". Put your monthly costs next to step 1 and find the three biggest you could cut for a year. Then write one skill businesses already pay for that you could learn by doing.
