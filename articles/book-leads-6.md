**The argument.** Paid ads rent other people's audiences: you trade money for guaranteed reach, so it's a game of efficiency, not reach. Narrow the whole world down to the people most likely to buy, make an ad they notice and want, get their contact details, and keep what each customer earns you at least three times what they cost to get.

## The story: the ugliest ad you've ever seen
- In 2013 Hormozi got a gym owner to bet $1,000 on Facebook ads. The ad was plain text in capitals: "I'm looking for five Chino Hills residents to take part in a free six-week challenge…"
- Leads came within hours. 19 people bought at $299, just under $5,700 from $1,000. The six-week challenge went on to drive at least $1.5 billion across the gym industry.

## Making an ad
- **Find the needle in the haystack.** Pick a platform where your buyers are (one you use yourself, can target on, know the ad format for, and can afford). Start where your competitors advertise: replicate before you iterate.
- **Target them.** A lookalike audience built from your customer list (topped up with your warm and cold lists to reach the minimum size), plus filters like age, place and interests. Win small, then widen from puddle to ocean.
- **Callout.** The first five seconds matter most; focusing there made Hormozi's ads 20 times more efficient. The cocktail-party effect: labels ("Irving moms", "gym owners"), yes-questions ("Do you wake up to pee more than once a night?"), if-then statements and ridiculous results. Then contrast, likeness (look like a plumber to reach plumbers) and the scene.
- **Record 30 callouts for 10 ads.** That makes 300 versions in a few hours. Keep the best callout and use it everywhere.
- **What, who, when.** The value: their dream outcome and the nightmare without you, how likely it is to work and the risk of doing nothing, how fast it is and how slow they are now, how easy it is and how hard their way is. Who sees the change: spouse, kids, boss, rivals. When: their past, present and future.
- **The call to action.** Spell out exactly what to do, in a short memorable address. Then a simple landing page that matches the ad, to collect their contact details.

## Money stuff
- **Three phases.** Track money (set up tracking first), lose money (nine ads lose, one wins five to one, then you put 100 times more behind it), print money (budget backwards from how many customers you want, plus 20%).
- **The testing budget.** Give a new ad up to twice the cash a customer brings in their first 30 days. No leads by one times that, switch it off.
- **LTGP to CAC.** Lifetime gross profit per customer against the cost to acquire one. Businesses that struggle to grow are below 3 to 1. If your CAC is within three times your industry's average, fix the business model rather than the ads: costs can only fall to zero, earnings can grow without limit.
- **Client-financed acquisition.** Make more in a customer's first 30 days than they cost to get and serve, and money stops being the limit. A $15-a-month membership ($10 profit) with a $30 CAC breaks even in 30 days with a $100 upsell one in five take.

## Four lessons
- Don't mistake a sales problem for an ad problem: if the right people get on the phone and don't buy, the ads work.
- Your best free content often makes your best paid ads, and so do customers' own reviews.
- Never say "I'm not techie": building a landing page took four years of avoiding and four hours of doing.
- Place your first ad for $100. You won't earn from it; you'll learn.

**The idea worth keeping.** All ads work; the only question is how well. Get the right people to notice in five seconds, and make sure a customer pays back what they cost within 30 days.

**Try it on your work.** Write three callouts for your AI service (a label, a yes-question and an if-then), then one short ad with the value and a call to action. Work out the most you could pay for a customer and still get 3 to 1.
