**The argument.** Engineers believe a great product sells itself. It doesn't. Poor sales, not a bad product, is the most common reason businesses fail. Sales is hidden on purpose (nobody wants to feel sold to), so people underrate it. If you can't get your product to people, you don't have a business.

## How products get sold, by price
| Method | Fits | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Complex sales | Very large deals, few customers | The founder sells personally, over months |
| Personal sales | Mid-sized deals | A small sales team builds a repeatable process |
| Marketing and advertising | Cheap products for many people | Ads and brand, where you can't afford a salesperson per customer |
| Viral | Products people share by using them | Each user brings more users (PayPal paid people to sign up friends) |

- **The gap in the middle:** products too expensive to sell with ads but too cheap for a salesperson to visit. Many businesses die there.
- **One channel wins.** Like the power law, a single distribution channel usually beats all the others combined. Find it and focus.
- **Everyone sells.** Founders sell to investors and recruits, employees sell the company to each other. You're always selling something.

**The idea worth keeping.** For a service, the method is almost always personal: you, talking to people. That's not a stopgap. It's the channel.

**Try it on your work.** Write how your first ten customers will hear about you: the actual channel, not "word of mouth". Then put a weekly number on it, like the rule of 100 on the Hormozi shelf. This is P09 and P10.
