**The argument.** Ask your mum if your business idea is good and she'll say yes, because she loves you. So will most people. It's not their fault: you asked the wrong question. A good question is one even your mum can't lie to you about, because it's about her life and her past, not your idea and her opinions.

## The three rules
1. **Talk about their life instead of your idea.**
2. **Ask about specifics in the past, not opinions or guesses about the future.**
3. **Talk less and listen more.**

## Bad questions and better ones
| Bad | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|
| "Do you think it's a good idea?" | Only the market can tell you that | "How do you deal with this now?" |
| "Would you buy something that did X?" | People are optimistic about their future selves | "When did this last happen? Talk me through it." |
| "How much would you pay for X?" | A guess about the future | "What have you already tried? What did it cost?" |
| "What would your dream product do?" | Fine to ask, but don't build it as told | "Why do you bother? What happens if you don't?" |

Two more good ones: "Who else should I talk to?" and "Is there anything else I should have asked?"

**The idea worth keeping.** Once you mention your idea, people start being nice instead of truthful. Keep it out of the conversation for as long as you can.

**Try it on your work.** Rewrite three questions you'd normally ask about your idea so they're about the person's last week instead. Then have one real conversation using only those. This is lesson P04.
