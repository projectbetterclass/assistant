**The claim.** Big Tech's borrowing for AI now drives long-term interest rates, not the Fed. When nobody seems to be steering, patient money moves into gold, and central banks are already buying a third of all new gold. Felix isn't telling you to buy, but he thinks doing nothing and sitting in cash is the riskiest choice.

## Why the Fed is "a passenger"
- Three senior Goldman Sachs traders said it on their own show: AI is now a bond market story, not just a stock story.
- AI debt reached nearly $500B by August, about a fifth of all quality US corporate borrowing. Next year it could be $1.3T, more than the US government borrows.
- The world's spare savings are limited. When tech, governments, defense and new factories all borrow at once, investors sell government bonds to buy the new debt, and long-term rates rise.
- The Fed only sets the overnight rate. Your mortgage and your portfolio follow the long-term rate, which the bond market sets.
- The spending comes now and the productivity payoff later, so Goldman calls the boom inflationary. A trader called the Treasury's $8–16B a week of bond buybacks "a firecracker in a hurricane".

## Why gold, according to Goldman's metals desk
| | Then | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Gold mined per year | ~3,500 tons | ~3,500 tons |
| Bought by central banks | 400–500 tons (before 2022) | over 1,000 tons |

- Central banks keep what they buy, so less gold is left for everyone else, and "you don't need as much investment capital to drive metals materially higher."
- Gold peaked in January and sits about 20% lower. The desk calls it "an elongated pause" caused by a new Fed chair (Kevin Warsh) and the Iran conflict. It sees $4,000 as a floor and says to scale in on dips toward it.
- They argue the old rule "higher rates hurt gold" is broken, because rates are rising out of fear about government finances.
- **Silver:** half of demand is industrial and only ~20% is investment, so it can run from $50 to $100, with 20–30% drops along the way. That's for traders, not for central banks.

**Watch for.** The video is a funnel for a free report and a live training called "the index fund trap", with no replays. He says 70% of an S&P 500 fund sits in 10 AI companies. In his other videos it's 40% of the index and 72% of this year's gains, which is closer. "Buffett sold their index fund" refers to two small S&P 500 fund stakes Berkshire sold in late 2024, a tiny part of its portfolio.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 11: gold −4.4% (about $4,220 an ounce, still above the $4,000 floor), silver −6.0%. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from 4.97% to 5.24%, so the rates part of the story held. Felix said he didn't expect a rate hike, but the Fed raised rates on Sep 16. The S&P 500 is up 1.9%.

**Aswath check.** Gold has no cash flows, so you can't value it, only price it. Aswath treats it as insurance against a crisis, not as an investment. The useful part for you is the bond story: if long-term rates stay higher, your riskfree rate (L03) and every discount rate built on it go up, and every valuation comes out lower.
