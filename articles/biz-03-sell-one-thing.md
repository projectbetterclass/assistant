**The idea.** From zero to $1M, sell one product to one kind of customer through one channel. Don't build a cheap, medium and premium menu. A confused mind doesn't buy, and whoever is selling won't know what to push. Make one offer that solves the whole problem, and flex the payment terms instead.

## Why one thing
- Many offers make the business messy. "If your employees don't understand all the things you sell, your customers for sure don't."
- 0 to $1M: one product, one avatar (one kind of customer), one channel. Add a second product line only around $1–3M, on the way to $10M: one thing up front, one for later.
- With three tiers the seller has to choose, and usually can't. With one thing, there's one thing to convince people of.

## Flex the payment, not the product
- People look at the monthly cost, not the total they're signing up for.
- His example, a six-month programme: pay in full, in two parts, in three (about 20% more in total), or monthly.
- The question stops being "which one do you want?" and becomes "how would you like to pay?" More ways to pay means more people buy.
- **"The bigger the head, the longer the tail."** A big payment up front and a small monthly after ($5,000 down, then $99 a month) keeps people far longer than $99 a month alone. The up-front cash also covers ads, commission and onboarding.

**Watch for.** He aims this at owners under about $3M who aren't strong operators. Charging more for spreading the payments is common, but say it plainly to the customer.

**Try it this week.** Write your service as one sentence: who it's for, the problem it solves, and in how long. Then write three ways to pay for it. If you catch yourself writing a second service, cut it. Ties to P07 and P08.
