**The idea.** One 14-minute slot every day is about 7 hours a month: enough to learn, send two messages a day and serve one or two clients. Running the Business and Products slots as this one project roughly doubles it, to about 14–16 hours, which covers four or five clients. Beyond that the business needs a weekly block, or the time you'll have after graduating.

## What fits
| Activity | Time it takes | Fits in 14 minutes a day? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn one tool or technique | 15–30 min a step | Yes |
| Two outreach messages or calls | 10–15 min | Yes |
| Post one note about your work (proof) | 10 min | Yes |
| 20 owner interviews | 15–20 hours over 4–6 weeks | No: use your graduation project or weekend blocks |
| Setting up a new client | about 8 hours | No: one weekend |
| Delivering for one client, by hand | about 3 hours a month | Just: 1–2 clients |
| Delivering for one client, automated | about 1.5 hours a month | Yes: 4–5 clients with two slots |

## Three ways to make the time
- **Use your graduation project.** If your final year includes a thesis or internship, aim it at AI adoption in your chosen niche. Your interviews become research, and the firm you work with can become your first client and case study.
- **Combine Business and Products into this branch.** They are the same project now; your notes on offers and first customers become the to-do list.
- **Decide what this is for.** Hormozi's "stop dabbling" (your first Business note) applies: eight daily things at 14 minutes each is a fine way to learn, and a slow way to build. After graduating, give the winner a bigger slot.

**Try it this week.** Decide whether this branch takes over the Business and Products slots in your weekday round, and block one weekend morning for interviews.
