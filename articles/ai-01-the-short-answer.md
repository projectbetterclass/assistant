**The short answer.** Start with an AI-powered service for one boring Dutch industry, sold as a fixed monthly package. Build software only after that service has shown you exactly what to automate. Your SaaS idea comes back in year 2, grown out of the service.

## Why this and not SaaS first
- **Small Dutch firms barely use AI yet.** 4–7% of construction and hospitality firms do, against 54% in tech.
- **Their staff are scarce and expensive.** An hour of staff time costs €47.90, the third highest in the EU. A service that saves 15 hours a month is worth about €700 to a firm.
- **Hormozi backs it, with a warning.** He calls AI implementation for small businesses "an enormous opportunity", but says most AI businesses lack basic business sense, and that software is a hard game for people who aren't software people.
- **Building got cheap; customers didn't.** 81 of the 187 Starter Story founders in your library built with AI coding or no-code tools. The scarce part was distribution.

## The path in four phases
1. **Discover, months 1–2.** 20 owner interviews, through your graduation project if you can, to find one weekly task. Go on when 6 of 10 owners name the same task, with hours attached.
2. **Pilot, months 2–4.** Three firms free for 60 days, done by hand, every edge case logged. Go on when a pilot client pays €300 or more a month.
3. **Paid service, months 4–12.** €500–1,500 a month per client; automate one step at a time. Go on when about 10 clients run on the same steps, under 1.5 hours each.
4. **Software, year 2 onward.** Turn the workflow those clients share into a tool for the industry. Later, buying a firm becomes an option.
