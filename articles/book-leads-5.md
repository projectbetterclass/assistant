**The argument.** Cold outreach is warm outreach to strangers: one to one, but with people who don't know or trust you. That brings three new problems, and you solve them in order: get a way to contact them, know what to say so they reply, and reach out often enough, in enough ways, to make up for the low response.

## The story: COVID, and a sales rep with no leads
- In 2020 a third of Hormozi's gym customers closed and ads stopped working. A sales rep whose job offer had just been pulled said the gym software company they'd worked for made $10 million a month from cold outreach alone.
- Hormozi gave them the runway to build a team. September: no sales. Then 2, 4, 6, 10, 14, 20 and 30 sales by May, $480,000 that month. The team asked twice to pull the plug.
- **The lessons.** A competitor can be far bigger than you know, because private advertising is invisible. Expect it to take about a year, not twelve weeks. And learn from someone who has done it before.

## Problem 1: a way to contact them, so build a list
- **You pick your targets.** Hedge fund managers running over $1 billion, golf shops over $3 million: cold outreach can be as specific as you like.
- **Three ways to a list.** Lead-scraping software (test a few hundred from each tool first), list brokers (test their samples), or elbow grease: join groups where your buyers are and collect them by hand. Hand-built lists are the freshest, since nobody else has them.
- **Interest groups.** The warmest cold audience you can get: people gathered around the problem you solve.

## Problem 2: what to say, so personalize and give big fast value
- **Personalize.** One to three things a friend might know about them, a compliment, and how it helped you. Make a cold message look warm. Rewriting emails below a third-grade reading level got 50% more replies.
- **Big fast value.** Strangers give you seconds, so give away the farm: something people normally pay for. Swapping a "game plan session" (a sales call in disguise) for free real work tripled the take rate.
- Scripts stay short: a page for calls and chat, half a page for email. Send the first 10,000 before you polish.

## Problem 3: not enough chances, so add volume
- **Automate delivery and distribution.** Record a voicemail or video once and send it to everyone; dial or email in batches. You trade some personalization for scale, so with a small market, personalize every one.
- **Follow up more times, more ways.** Email first, then call "about my email". Expect two or three conversations before a big sale. If someone asks you to stop, stop.
- **Start back at the top in three to six months.** Circumstances change: someone fine before the holidays may need you after them.
- **Replicate before you iterate.** New to a team? Find the best rep and double their numbers.

## The numbers
- **Benchmarks.** Phone: 100 calls, 20% pick up, 25% of those take the lead magnet, four engaged leads in four hours. Email: aim for about 3% of the list becoming engaged leads.
- **What a customer costs.** $15 an hour plus $50 a show, 200 calls a day, two shows: $120 a show. Close a third and a client costs $360, against $3,600 profit: 10 to 1. You want at least 3 to 1.
- **Why bother.** It's private (competitors can't see it), reliable (for every X contacted you get Y customers), rarely changed by platform rules, works without you on camera (which made Gym Launch sellable), and is hard to copy.
- **Count in hundreds.** At least 100 reach-outs a day, for at least 100 days. For low-priced products, reach affiliates instead of customers.

**The idea worth keeping.** Strangers don't trust you, so earn it in seconds: a personal opener, then something they'd normally pay for, sent to a good list over and over again.

**Try it on your work.** Build a list of 50 small firms that fit your AI service. For five of them, write the personal opener and the big fast value you'd give away. Send them, then follow up by another channel.
