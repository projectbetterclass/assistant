**The argument.** When the Core Four aren't bringing enough leads, there are only three things to do to any of them: do more of what works, make it better, then take it somewhere new. Most people stop because they think they've used up their market; almost always they've barely touched it.

## The story: "I've saturated the market"
- A chiropractic business owner at $2 million a year said the niche was saturated, after spending $30,000 a month on one platform, with no content, no cold outreach and no tracking, in a $15 billion industry.
- A second owner in the same niche said they'd spent $30,000 across four platforms in the past week. There are more leads out there than you can imagine.

## More
- **Double the inputs.** Even with no improvements, twice the reach-outs, posts or ad spend gets more engaged leads. The biggest gains often come from more.
- **The Rule of 100.** 100 primary actions a day for 100 days in a row: 100 reach-outs, or 100 minutes making content (at least one post a day), or 100 minutes making ads at your daily budget.
- **More ads make better ads.** Facebook found its top 0.1% of advertisers tested 11 times more creative than everyone else. You often can't scale a mediocre ad; you find the exceptional one by making many.

## Better
- **Fix the constraint.** Every step a lead takes is a place they drop off. Improve the step with the biggest drop: lifting 5% to 10% doubles your leads, while lifting 30% to 35% adds only 16%.
- **One test a week, per platform.** Change one thing, run it a week, keep the winner, write it in a test log, and try to beat it again. Can't beat it in four tries? Move to the next constraint.
- **Front to back.** The lowest percentages are usually at the front: the callout, the offer, the headline, the landing page.
- **Better, more, new.** Under $1 million a year in profit, do more first. Above that, making things better is often the cheapest, highest-return move.

## New
- **The size-of-the-pie fallacy.** Seeing three or four competitors in your corner of one platform, you assume they split the whole market. Your slice of one platform isn't the market.
- **When to go new.** When more and better return less than the same effort somewhere new. Order: a new placement (stories, shorts), then a new platform, then a new Core Four activity.
- **They boost each other.** Content makes ads and outreach work better; outreach warms your content audience. Hormozi built each business on a different pair. Pick one method, max it out, then add the next.

**The idea worth keeping.** Ask what's stopping you from doing ten times what you do now. Usually nothing: do more until it breaks, fix the weakest step, and only then go somewhere new.

**Try it on your work.** Pick one Core Four activity for your AI service and set your Rule of 100: what counts as one primary action, and the 100 days. Then name the step where most of your leads drop off.
