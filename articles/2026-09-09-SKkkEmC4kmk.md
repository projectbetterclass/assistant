**The claim.** The US government is about to buy back its own long-dated bonds, twice as fast, for two months (until the Nov 4 midterms), because the biggest foreign buyer is selling. Felix calls it money printing in disguise and a hidden tax on your savings. Own things that can't be printed: businesses that can raise prices, and a sensible slice of gold. Don't sit in cash, and don't panic-sell US stocks.

## The story he tells
- **The buyback:** a line in the Treasury's refunding statement doubles the purchases of long bonds. Markets now expect the Fed to *raise* rates toward 4%, so one foot is on the gas and one on the brake.
- **Japan:** Japanese holdings of US debt fell by about $88–90B in August, the most ever. About 70% of Japan's reserves are US debt. When money goes home to defend the yen, it stops propping up US bonds and stocks.
- **Borrowing everywhere:** Big Tech borrows ~$700B this year and ~$1T next year for data centers, on top of $2T+ of government debt, and the pool of buyers is shrinking.
- **Gold:** the Netherlands, France and Germany are bringing their gold home. Physical gold is now one of America's biggest exports. Trump posted "he who has the gold makes the rules."
- **Inflation:** $100 bought 125 bottles of Coke in 2000 and buys 44 today. A 1971 dollar is worth 7 cents.
- **What insiders do:** Trump's disclosure of ~600 trades leans into "boring" stocks like Berkshire, Visa, Mastercard, Home Depot and Republic Services, and away from AI.
- **Your index fund:** 72% of the S&P 500's gains this year came from the top 10 AI names, so it's less diversified than it looks.

**Watch for.** Treasury buybacks aren't printing money. The Treasury can't print; it pays for buybacks by issuing short-term bills, which swaps long debt for short. The Fed is the one that creates money. He also says "no links, no affiliates" and then pitches his free report, a live training and a free trial of his politician-trade app.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 9: gold −5.5%. The president's five "boring" stocks average +0.3% (Visa +4.9%, Home Depot −6.3%), against +2.3% for the S&P 500. Coca-Cola, his pricing-power example, is +0.6%. The 10-year yield did rise, from 4.84% to 5.24%.

**Aswath check.** "Companies that can raise prices" is a real valuation idea, but you have to show it in the numbers: steady margins through inflation and a high return on capital (L10, L20). Before calling Coca-Cola a hedge, check its operating margin in 2021–2023, when inflation hit.
