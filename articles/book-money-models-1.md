**The argument.** Hormozi got rich by making more money from customers than it cost to get them, and making it fast. A money model is the sequence of offers that does it: offer the right thing at the moment a customer needs it, again and again. Get your costs back within 30 days, and the cost of getting customers stops being a problem.

## The storage man and the $5 lead
- **A free month that costs $127.** Sleeping on the gym floor with a month of cash left, Hormozi was taken to breakfast by a storage-unit owner. The free first month came with a lock only that facility sells ($47), boxes, tape and labels, moving kit, insurance upgrades and the next size up, because everyone rents too small.
- **A level-10 skill in a level-2 opportunity.** Years later, with six gyms, Hormozi paid $25,000 for an hour with a famous marketer. The gyms made about $34 in 48 hours for every $1 of ads ($5 leads, a $600 programme, supplements, then members rolling into a year). The advice: stop running gyms and teach other owners. That became Gym Launch, sold years later at a $46.2 million valuation.

## What a money model is
- **The rental car.** A $19-a-day car became $100 a day: a roomier truck, late return, premium insurance (declined), a minimum-insurance downsell, and prepaid fuel. Every offer solved a problem Hormozi was about to have.
- **A sequence of offers.** Find every chance to solve a customer's problem, and offer to solve it, in a specific order. Offer the right thing when they realize they need it, and you can make as many offers as you like.
- **Bad money models kill businesses.** If a customer costs more to get than they bring in early on, you cut ads, borrow, sell pieces of the business, and wait months or years to get paid back. $100 of ads for $500 of profit is a great deal, unless it takes two years to come in.
- **The 30-day rule.** Cover the cost of getting a customer within 30 days. A credit card gives you 30 days of interest-free money; pay it back from the customer, then do it again.
- **Why it compounds.** Customers twice as valuable, twice as many of them, twice as fast: the business grows eight times faster. Three times each: 27 times.

## The four types of offers
- **Attraction offers** get customers in.
- **Upsell offers** sell them more, so you finally make some cash.
- **Downsell offers** turn their "no" into a "yes" at a different price or with less.
- **Continuity offers** bring in cash every month.
- They work alone, in any order, but Hormozi's most profitable businesses use all four.

## The rules that come with it
- Every business has a money model: ask "how will I make this work for my business?", not "this won't work for my business".
- Design your own; copying someone else's disappoints.
- If a customer asks for their money back, give it back. Hard selling is for weak products: offer at the moment of the problem, and if they don't want it, find someone who does.
- Obey the law (check offers with a lawyer, especially anything "free") and be transparent: if the facts aren't compelling, change the facts.

**The idea worth keeping.** The best businesses don't make one sale; they solve the next problem the moment it appears, and get their cost of a customer back within 30 days.

**Try it on your work.** List every problem a small firm has right after buying your AI service. Put them in the order they show up, and next to each, the offer that would solve it.
