**The argument.** A money model is a deliberate sequence of offers: what you offer, when, and how, to make as much as you can as fast as you can. It's built in three stages, each paying for the next: get cash (attraction), get more cash (upsells and downsells), get the most cash (continuity). Try to build it all at once and it collapses on you.

## How Gym Launch's money model grew
- **Stage one, a decoy.** Free books, courses and training on growing a gym, each with a free call: do it yourself for free, or get help putting it in place for $16,000 over 16 weeks. That reached $476,000 a month within three months, then risked a plateau with only one thing to sell.
- **Stage two, an upsell and its downsells.** "Gym Lords", $42,000 a year for advanced playbooks, services and a community, or $36,000 prepaid. For a no: $10,000 down and the rest over time; then $800 a week for 52 weeks; then start free, with the free time lasting until the first programme ended. $1.5 million a month.
- **Stage three, a menu and feature downsells.** Done-for-you ads, sales training, turnkey campaigns, down to a minimum package of the original materials with tech support (from $800 a week to $100). Almost everyone stayed for something: $2.3 million a month within 14 months, and $4.4 million by month 20 with a supplement company added.
- **It only looks clean looking back.** None of Hormozi's businesses started with a finished money model, Acquisition.com included.

## Build it one stage at a time
- **The order.** Get customers reliably. Make them pay for themselves reliably, then for other customers. Then maximize each customer's long-term value. Then spend as much on ads as you can.
- **Fair warning.** When your money model starts working, your business starts breaking. Find someone who can build and lead the team.
- **There are many $100M money models.** These are just the ones Hormozi has used.

## Four example models
- **Services (Gym Launch).** Decoy, then classic upsell, payment plan downsells, and a menu with feature downsells.
- **Local business (a small gym).** Win your money back on a fitness challenge, with split payments and a trial with penalty as downsells; a supplement menu upsell ("you won't get the best results without the right supplements") with big and small bundles; then a rollover into membership, $50 off a month for a 12-month commitment.
- **Digital product (a newsletter).** A free trial, then monthly; or pay less now and keep that rate for life. One offer working as attraction, upsell and continuity at once.
- **Physical product (dog food).** Buy four months, get two free; toys and vitamins as classic upsells; "just the premium food then?" as the downsell; automatic renewal after six months.

## Make your own, in four steps
1. **An attraction offer**, to turn strangers into customers and cover your costs. Finding what works can take up to a year.
2. **An upsell offer**, to get 30-day profit well above the cost of getting and serving a customer. Solve the problem the first offer creates, at the moment of greatest need.
3. **A downsell offer**, to turn a no into a yes: keep the price and change how they pay (payment plans, trials), or change what they get (feature downsells). Alternate in the same sale.
4. **A continuity offer**, for one last sale in the 30 days and recurring cash. Sometimes the right moment is after day 30; better then than forced.

## Rules for building it
- **Perfect one offer at a time.** Run it until it's reliable, then until it's automatic, then move on. Measure in quarters, not weeks: you either build it right or build it again.
- **Raise prices in stages.** Start cheap to get yeses and feedback, then raise the price until the extra cash from the yeses no longer covers the noes.
- **Simple scales, fancy fails.** Not 100 products: 100 ways to offer your product (one to four training sessions a week).
- **Affiliate products fill gaps.** Sell someone else's stuff for a commission: a dental agency sends clients to a braces maker, a massage therapist sells home tools shipped by someone else, an educator recommends software.
- **Roll attraction into continuity.** "Buy six months, get six free" can renew month to month at the end.
- **Mix and match.** Upsell tactics in an attraction offer, a downsell after every offer, continuity to attract. Start the way the book shows, then experiment.

## Ten years in ten minutes
- **The three books.** $100M Offers: what to sell (an offer so good people feel stupid saying no). $100M Leads: how to find the people (advertise). $100M Money Models: how to get them to buy (a sequence of offers).
- **The bare minimum.** A good money model makes more profit from a customer in 30 days than they cost to get and serve. A $100M one makes enough to get and serve many more, so cash stops limiting growth.
- **The offers.** Attraction: win your money back, giveaways, decoys, buy X get Y free, pay less now or more later. Upsell: classic, menu, anchor, rollover. Downsell: payment plans, trial with penalty, feature downsells. Continuity: bonuses, discounts, waived fees.
- **The send-off.** Confidence comes from a stack of undeniable proof, not affirmations. "You cannot lose if you do not quit." Follow the lessons and the money will come.

**The idea worth keeping.** Build one offer until it works without you thinking about it, then add the next. Each stage should pay for the one after it.

**Try it on your work.** Name the stage your AI service is at right now, the one offer you'll make reliable this quarter, and the price you'll start it at.
