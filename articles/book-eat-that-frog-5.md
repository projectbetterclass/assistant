**The argument.** Big tasks get put off because they feel too big. Get everything ready so there's no excuse, look only as far as the next step, and break the task into pieces small enough to start right now. Once you start, finishing pulls you along.

## Prepare thoroughly before you begin
- **Everything to hand.** Before you start, have every paper, file and tool you'll need, so you don't stop to look for things.
- **A clean workspace.** Clear the desk of everything except the task. A tidy, comfortable place to work makes it easier to sit down and stay.
- **Then launch.** Once you're set up, start straight away, before the preparation turns into another way to put it off.

## One oil barrel at a time
- **The Sahara.** As a young man, Tracy crossed the Sahara. On long stretches the only route markers were empty oil barrels a few kilometres apart. Nobody had to see across the desert, only to reach the next barrel.
- **The lesson.** You don't need to see the whole way to the goal. Take the next step, then the one after it.

## Slice and dice the task
- **The salami slice.** Cut a big task into thin slices and do just one. Finishing a slice makes you want to do the next: people have a drive to complete things.
- **Swiss cheese.** Punch a hole in the task: work on it for a set time, say 5 or 10 minutes, then stop. Starting is the hardest part, and often you'll keep going.
- **Finishing feels good.** Tracy says completing a task gives you a natural lift that makes the next one easier.

**The idea worth keeping.** You don't have to see the whole way: get ready, then take one slice, and let finishing it pull you to the next.

**Try it on your work.** Take tomorrow's frog for your AI service. Tonight, put everything it needs in one place. Tomorrow, set a 10-minute timer and do the first slice.
