**The argument.** Big tasks get put off because you're not ready, because they look too big, or because you don't feel good enough at them. Get everything ready before you begin, look only as far as the next step, and keep upgrading the skills your key tasks need: the better you are at something, the faster you start it.

## Prepare thoroughly before you begin
- **Like cooking a meal.** Set all the ingredients out first. Clear the desk so only this task is in front of you, and have everything you need in reach: papers, logins, materials.
- **A workspace you like.** Clean, neat and comfortable, with a good chair. Many books never get written because the preparation never happened.
- **Then launch.** Do the first thing. Tracy's rule: get it 80% right, then correct it later. Sit up straight, sit forward, say "let's get to work", and plunge in.

## Take it one oil barrel at a time
- **The Sahara.** As a young person, Tracy drove an old Land Rover across the Tanezrouft, 500 miles without water or features, where more than 1,300 people had died. The French had marked the track with black oil drums every five kilometres: exactly the distance to the horizon. All you had to do was steer for the next barrel.
- **The lesson.** Go as far as you can see, and you'll see far enough to go further. Financial independence comes from saving a little every month; fitness from eating a little less and moving a little more.
- **Exercise.** List all the steps of a task you've been putting off, and do one now.

## Upgrade your key skills
- **Why it matters.** A major reason for procrastination is feeling inadequate at a key part of the task. However good you are, your skills are going out of date.
- **Tracy's typing.** Writing the first book by hunt and peck was slow, so Tracy practised touch typing 20 to 30 minutes a day for three months, reached 40 to 50 words a minute, and went on to write more than 80 books.
- **Three steps to mastery.** Read in your field for at least an hour a day. Take every course and seminar on your key skills. Listen to audio in the car: drivers spend 500 to 1,000 hours a year at the wheel.
- **Special talents.** Focus on what you're very good at, or could be, and enjoy most.

**The idea worth keeping.** Get everything ready, take the next step you can see, and keep getting better at the key tasks, so starting them gets easier.

**Try it on your work.** Take tomorrow's frog for your AI service. Tonight, put everything it needs in one place and list its steps. Tomorrow, do the first step, and name one skill it needs that you'll spend 20 minutes a day on this month.
