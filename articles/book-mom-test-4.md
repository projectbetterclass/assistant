**The argument.** A meeting where everyone is friendly and nothing is agreed is a failure dressed as a success. Real interest shows up as commitment: the person gives up something they value. And a good conversation ends with a next step that moves you forward.

## The three currencies of commitment
| Currency | Looks like |
|---|---|
| Time | A follow-up meeting with a clear goal; trying the thing; giving detailed feedback |
| Reputation risk | Introducing you to their boss, a colleague or a customer; a public testimonial |
| Money | A deposit, a pre-order, a letter of intent, a paid pilot |

## Reading a conversation
- **Compliments without commitment = zombie lead.** "Sounds great, keep me posted" means no.
- **Every meeting either advances or it doesn't.** Know before you start what next step you're hoping for.
- **Ask for a commitment.** If they refuse, you've learned something. If you never ask, you learn nothing.
- **Early customers are the ones who commit early,** often because the problem hurts and they've already tried to solve it themselves.

**The idea worth keeping.** Judge every conversation by one test: did they give up time, reputation or money?

**Try it on your work.** Decide what commitment you'll ask for in your next conversation: a second chat, an intro to someone, a small deposit, a free trial on the conditions in the Hormozi first-five read. Ask for it. This is P06.
