**The argument.** The gold has stopped flowing in Babylon and most people have nothing to show for their earnings, so the king asks Arkad to teach the city. Arkad teaches a class of a hundred in the Temple of Learning, one cure a day for seven days. Together they turn a lean purse into a fat one: keep a tenth, live on a budget, make the savings earn and keep them safe, own your home, provide for the future, and grow what you can earn.

## Cures one and two: keep a tenth, live on the rest
- **Start thy purse to fattening.** Put ten eggs in a basket every morning and take out nine every evening, and the basket overflows. For every ten coins in your purse, take out only nine.
- **Control thy expenditures.** What we call "necessary expenses" always grow to equal our income, unless we fight it. Don't confuse them with desires: everyone has more desires than income. List what you want, keep the things you need within nine-tenths of your income, and cross out the rest without regret.

## Cures three and four: make it earn, keep it safe
- **Make thy gold multiply.** Arkad's first good loan was to Aggar the shield maker, lending back the rental each time, so both the capital and its earnings grew. Your wealth isn't the coins in your purse but the stream of income they bring in.
- **Guard thy treasures from loss.** Safety of the principal comes first. Before investing, ask people experienced in handling money, and don't be tempted by big promised returns.

## Cures five to seven: home, future, earning power
- **Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment.** Own your home: the money that would have gone to a landlord builds something of your own.
- **Insure a future income.** Provide for when you can no longer earn, and for your family if you die. A farmer who left a small sum with a moneylender for a child found it had grown by compound interest when the child turned 20.
- **Increase thy ability to earn.** Want something specific: five pieces of gold, then ten, then twenty. Get more skilled at your work: Arkad became one of the fastest tablet carvers by taking more interest and concentrating harder. Pay your debts promptly, look after your family, make a will, help people in misfortune, and respect yourself.

**The idea worth keeping.** Keep a tenth, live on the rest by a budget, make the tenth earn and keep it safe, own your home, provide for the future, and keep growing what you can earn.

**Try it on your work.** Score your own money from 1 to 10 against each of the seven cures, using your spending view and FIRE plan. Pick the lowest score, and write one step you'll take on it this month.
