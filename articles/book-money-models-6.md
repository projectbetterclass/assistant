**The argument.** Continuity offers give ongoing value for ongoing payments until the customer cancels: you sell once and get paid again and again. They bring in less cash today, which makes them hard to advertise on their own, so Hormozi makes them last: attraction, upsell and downsell offers bring in the cash, then continuity stacks the recurring revenue.

## Why continuity, and why last
- **The maths.** A $1,000 offer to 100 people: 10 buy, $10,000 now and nothing later. The same thing at $50 a month: 40 buy and stay 20 months, $2,000 now and $40,000 over time, with four times as many customers to upsell.
- **Only for ongoing value.** Nobody should pay forever for a one-day workshop; that's a payment plan. But if the value keeps coming, the payments should too.
- **No standalone memberships.** Nobody wants a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried. Every successful continuity business Hormozi has seen gets people in with something else, then upsells features and prepaid blocks of time.
- **Three offers.** Bonuses, discounts and waived fees, to get customers to start and to stay.

## Continuity bonus offers: get this free when you join
- **The story.** A gym marketing six-week challenges stopped selling the challenge: "want it for free?" Free if you become a member, plus member-only bonuses, then a discount for prepaying six months. Before, 34 in 100 bought the challenge and 17 stayed on; now 15 bought the challenge, 40 joined straight away and 8 of those prepaid. Membership sales tripled.
- **How it works.** Give something good for signing up today, usually worth more than the first payment. Advertise the bonus, not the membership.
- **Pick the right bonus.** Related to the core offer, or you attract the wrong people (a free t-shirt sells t-shirt printing, not tech services). Things you already have or do: back issues, onboarding. A physical bonus for a digital product, or the other way round (live-streamed classes for a boxing gym). Realistic values: a made-up anchor costs trust. Titles cost nothing: after three, six and twelve months, silver, gold, diamond. One friend's customers introduced themselves by their title.
- **Present it.** Sell the bonus and its value, let it shock them, then ask "do you want to know how to get this for free?" Stack more bonuses on top, each with its dollar value.
- **One-time vs. continuity pricing.** Some people always pay up front. Price a one-time option at 1.33 times the first month and half still choose continuity; at twice, 70%; at 2.66 times, 90%.
- **Bulk prepay.** "Buy five months, get one free": if one in eight takes it, 30-day profit rises 50%.

## Continuity discount offers: sign up today, get time free
- **The story.** A neighbour in Austin got into commercial trash collection by offering apartment complexes a whole year free for a five-year contract. Nobody would back them; after the first year, the cash flooded in, and years later they sold the business.
- **Where the discount goes.** Up front (best where contracts are enforced; it gets customers but delays cash). At the end, earned by paying on time. Spread over the term: three free months at $200 is $600, or $50 off each month for a year, kept for life if they pay on time. After the first payment or two (first and last month up front, or an activation fee).
- **Bill every four weeks.** A year has 13 four-week cycles: 8.3% more revenue for the same sale. At a 20% margin that's 41% more profit. Hormozi's highest-value-per-word note in the book.
- **Extend the term, don't eat into it.** Pay 12 months, get 3 free: 15 months.
- **A 3% processing fee.** Nobody has refused to buy over it, and at a 10% margin it adds 30% to profit. Offer to drop it for a second form of payment, ideally a bank transfer (ACH), which fixes expired and maxed-out cards.
- **Gift cards and earned discounts.** Give the free time as a gift card they can pass to a friend (a referral) or forget (a full-price sign-up). Offer a lifetime discount that starts at your most common cancellation month: a rice company gave 5% off a subscription, and 15% for life after five months.
- **Cancellations.** Make the cancellation fee equal to the discount they got. Make it obvious how to cancel, or they'll complain outside the business instead. Offer to waive the fee for an exit interview, get angrier about the problem than they are, and solve it or roll them into something better: a third to half get saved.

## Waived fee offers: month to month with a fee, or commit and skip it
- **The story.** A legendary high-ticket seller with no employees: "month to month with a big setup fee, or commit to a year and I'll waive it." Customers initial that leaving early means paying the fee. Early on, quitting costs a lot; later, it costs about the same as staying.
- **How it works.** A setup fee of three to five times the monthly rate: $1,000 a month, $5,000 to start month to month, or nothing for a 12-month commitment, paid only if they leave early.
- **Why it works.** People avoid fees, so they commit, and they stay for the same reason: if the cost to quit exceeds the cost to stay, they'll probably stay.
- **Explain it simply.** "It costs us money to get you started. If you're testing it, you cover that; if you commit, I will."
- **Tune it.** If more than 5% want to cancel early, look at the product: you want to nudge people, not handcuff them. For more cash up front, a smaller fee (1.5 to 3 times the monthly rate). The fee goes once they finish the commitment. Best for a year or more, and slow-results services like SEO, investing and weight loss.

**The idea worth keeping.** Get the cash up front with your other offers, then make staying the easy choice: a bonus to start, a discount for committing, and a fee that makes leaving cost more than staying.

**Try it on your work.** Write a continuity offer for your AI service: the monthly service, a bonus for joining today that you already have, and the setup fee you'd waive for a 12-month commitment.
