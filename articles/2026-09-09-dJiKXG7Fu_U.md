**The claim.** Tom Lee (Fundstrat) expected a September pullback, then flipped: everyone already fears a rate hike, so if the Fed simply holds on Sep 16, stocks "could rally very strongly." Carbutt walks through why that matters for tech stocks and where the call could break.

## Lee's calls
- **September:** the Fed holds and markets rally. The correction moves to October, from above 8,000 on the S&P 500 down to 7,300–7,400.
- **Year end:** 8,200 on the S&P 500 is "still a low number". His 2027 earnings estimate rose from $350 to $415 a share, with $425 possible.
- **The economy:** growth above 3%, from investment, onshoring and energy projects.
- **Crypto:** the four-year cycle ends this month. If the Clarity Act passes, Q4 is huge for Bitcoin and Ethereum. He keeps a $150,000 Bitcoin target.

## Why rates hit tech hardest
A tech stock's value sits mostly in profits years away. Higher rates shrink what those future profits are worth today, so a company can report great results and still fall. If the Fed hikes, he expects the damage in three waves:
1. The cash-rich giants (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta) get lower valuations.
2. AI chips and infrastructure fall harder.
3. Small, debt-funded growth companies get hit hardest.

**Carbutt's six doubts.** Lee needs a hold that the market doesn't expect. He's often right on direction and wrong on timing: he missed 8,000 in August and moved the correction before September ended. "Already priced in" works until it doesn't. A hold could be read as the Fed falling behind, so long yields rise anyway. Local fights over data centers can't be fixed by any rate decision. And a hike lands on a market crowded into AI names. The last minutes are an ad for his agency and courses.

**How the call aged.** The Fed **raised** rates on Sep 16, to 3.75–4.00%, so Lee's base case missed. Stocks rose anyway: the S&P 500 is up 2.3% since Sep 9, at about 7,810, without passing 8,000 or dropping to his October lows. The 10-year yield climbed to 5.24%. Bitcoin is up 5.6%, at about $82,600.

**Aswath check.** Carbutt's rate explanation is the discount-rate half of every valuation (L07, L24). A 25-point hike only matters if it moves the long-term riskfree rate (L03), and that's why he says to watch the 10- and 30-year yields, not the headline. Lee's $415 earnings estimate is a forecast.
