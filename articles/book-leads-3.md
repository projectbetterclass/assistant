**The argument.** There are only four ways to let anyone know about anything: you reach people who know you or people who don't, one at a time or many at once. The first of those, contacting people who know you one by one, is the cheapest and most reliable way to get your first five customers, and most businesses never do it.

## The Core Four
- **Warm and cold.** A warm audience has given you permission to contact them (friends, followers, customers, your contacts); a cold one hasn't (strangers, other people's audiences).
- **One to one, or one to many.** Private (a call, an email) or public (a post, a billboard). Automation doesn't change which one it is: emailing 10,000 people is still one to one, just fast.
- **The four.** Warm and one to one: warm outreach. Warm and one to many: posting content. Cold and one to one: cold outreach. Cold and one to many: paid ads. Not enough leads means you're not doing these with enough skill or volume.

## Warm outreach in ten steps
1. **Get your list.** Your phone, every email account, every social profile. Add them up; for many people it's the first thousand leads.
2. **Pick a platform.** The one with the most contacts.
3. **Personalize it.** Open with something real about them ("Saw you just had a baby, congrats"). You aren't asking for anything yet.
4. **100 a day.** Up to three tries each, a day apart. The first message is the hardest.
5. **ACA.** Acknowledge what they said, Compliment them, Ask a question that steers toward your offer.
6. **Make an offer, by asking for referrals.** "Do you know anyone who…?", built on the value equation: their dream outcome, fast, with little effort, plus a testimonial from someone like them. You never ask them to buy, so it isn't pushy, and many say "me".
7. **Make it free.** The first five free, in return for using it, giving feedback and a review. You get the practice, the forgiveness and the testimonials. If they still say no, ask why: the hidden costs (time, effort, sacrifice) are too high.
8. **Start back at the top.** Then the next platform. A thousand contacts is about a month of work with follow-ups.
9. **Start charging.** Once people refer others: 80% off for the next five, then 60%, then 40%, raising the price every five.
10. **Keep the list warm.** Give value regularly, then ask the nine-word email: "Are you still looking to [get what they want]?" Whoever replies goes to the top of the list.

## The numbers
- **One in five.** About one in five contacts replies; one in five of those takes the free offer; one of those four pays. So roughly one customer per 100 reach-outs.
- **What it's worth.** 500 a week at 1% buying a $400 offer is five customers a week: about $100,000 a year from this alone.
- **The limits.** Time (four hours a day, first thing) and the number of people who know you. Content solves the second.

**The idea worth keeping.** Start with the people who already know you: a personal message, a question about someone they know, and your first five for free. It always works if you do enough of it.

**Try it on your work.** Count your contacts across your phone, email and LinkedIn. Write the ACA reply and the "do you know anyone who…" offer for your AI service, then send the first ten.
