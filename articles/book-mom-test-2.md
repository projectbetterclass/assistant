**The argument.** A conversation can feel great and teach you nothing. Three kinds of bad data cause most of it: compliments, fluff, and ideas. Each feels like progress and isn't. The fix is to spot them and steer back to facts about the person's life.

## The three kinds
| Bad data | Sounds like | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Compliments | "That sounds really cool!" | Deflect it and get back to their situation. A compliment is worth nothing. |
| Fluff | "I usually…", "I always…", "I would definitely…", "I might…" | Anchor it to a real event: "When did that last happen?" |
| Ideas and feature requests | "You should add…" | Dig under it: "Why do you want that? What would it let you do?" |

## Other traps
- **Pitching.** If you catch yourself selling, stop. It turns the conversation into a test of your pitch, and they'll be polite.
- **Looking for approval.** If you leave a conversation feeling good, ask what you actually learned. Often it was a compliment.
- **Bad news is good news.** Learning that your idea doesn't work, before building it, saves you months.

**The idea worth keeping.** "Would", "might" and "always" are warning words. Real facts sound like "last Tuesday, I…".

**Try it on your work.** After your next conversation, write down everything you learned and mark each line: fact, compliment, fluff or idea. Throw out all but the facts. This is P05.
