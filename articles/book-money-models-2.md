**The argument.** Attraction offers turn advertising into customers by offering something free or discounted. Strangers can only take your word on value, but they understand price, so a great deal gets them in. You make money from free stuff because people come for one thing and buy another; attraction offers make that happen on purpose.

## Free and discount are the same tool
- **Free, discount or $1.** Wherever the book says free, you can use a discount or $1, and the other way round. The greatest discount of all is free.
- **Five attraction offers.** Win your money back, giveaways, the decoy offer, buy X get Y free, and pay less now or pay more later. The first two are this part.

## Win your money back
- **The story.** A gym owner's client wouldn't buy, so they made a deal: $500 for eight weeks, all of it back if they hit their goal, and the gym could use the results. They hit it, spent the money on more training, and posting the before-and-after brought 13 referrals. Hormozi's version of it has driven about $1 billion in sales.
- **How it works.** The customer pays now. Hit the goal (results), do what you're told (actions), or both, and they get it back as cash or store credit.
- **Good criteria.** Easy to track (phones already count steps), likely to get results (whatever your best customers do, make everyone do it), and advertising for you (posts, tags, reviews, referrals).
- **It works for anything people start and quit.** Starting a business, learning a skill, losing weight, self-care. B2B example: five customers in five days, money back if you send 100 messages a day, report your numbers and attend the trainings.
- **Store credit beats cash.** Offering credit got as many customers as cash back. Spread the credit over a bigger purchase: $600 won against a $200-a-month plan becomes $50 off every month for a year.
- **Every meeting is a chance to offer.** Make check-ins a condition of the refund, then offer what fits: supplements at the first meeting, a membership at the check-in and again at the end.
- **Make everyone a winner.** Halfway through, credit the programme toward the long-term plan whether they hit the goal or not. If someone fails and refuses, credit their deposit anyway: "you finished what you started".
- **The rules.** Only use it if your refund rate is below 5% (in Hormozi's gym data about 10% of customers asked for their money back). If someone wants a refund, give it. The real money comes from people who succeed and have something else to buy.

## Giveaways
- **The story.** A fitness certification company advertised one full-ride scholarship. Everyone who applied but didn't win got a "partial scholarship", and most joined on the spot. They had to cap it to keep up.
- **How it works.** Advertise a chance to win your big prize (the thing you want everyone to buy, with its value stated) in exchange for contact details. Pick the winner, then offer everyone else the same thing at a discount.
- **The steps.** Grand prize, promotional offer (a discount, bonus or slightly different version, called a scholarship, gift card or voucher), contact details and eligibility questions ("Why should we pick you?"), qualifying actions, a deadline of three to seven days with daily updates, then a public winner and private calls with everyone else.
- **The maths.** A $5,000-value prize retailing at $2,000, offered to everyone else at $1,800: a 10% discount that reads as $5,000 of value for $1,800.
- **Make the grand prize grand.** Event tickets drew almost nothing; a $50,000 bundle of well-known gym equipment plus a year of the product crushed it. Two prizes double the leads: if someone you refer wins, you win too.
- **Scarcity and urgency.** Cap entries at what you can call in seven days. Deadlines to enter, to claim and to use. Have a downsell ready for anyone who can't take the main offer.
- **Check the law.** Sweepstakes are heavily regulated; someone really has to win, and the rules must be clear.

**The idea worth keeping.** People come for the free thing and stay for what comes next: give them a real way to win, make every meeting a chance to offer more, and make everyone a winner.

**Try it on your work.** Design a "win your money back" pilot for your AI service: what a small firm pays up front, the two or three actions or results that win it back, and what the credit rolls into.
