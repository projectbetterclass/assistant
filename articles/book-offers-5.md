**The argument.** A Grand Slam Offer isn't one clever idea. It's a method: list every obstacle between the customer and their dream outcome, turn each into a solution, choose how to deliver each one, then keep the solutions that are worth the most and cost you the least.

## The five steps
1. **Name the dream outcome,** in the customer's words.
2. **List every problem** they'll hit on the way: before, during and after. Think through each step they'd take, and what could make them quit or doubt.
3. **Turn each problem into a solution.** Reword it: "I don't have time to cook" becomes "how to eat well with ten minutes a day".
4. **Choose how to deliver each solution,** with the delivery cube from the "what to sell" read on the Hormozi shelf: one-to-one or group, done for them or with them, live or recorded, and how fast.
5. **Trim and stack.** Cut what's costly to you and not worth much to them. Keep what's cheap for you and valuable to them. Stack what's left into one offer.

## Why it works
- You end up solving problems competitors don't even mention, so you stop being comparable.
- Each solution raises one of the four levers in the value equation.
- The list of problems is also your sales script: every objection already has an answer built in.

**The idea worth keeping.** The best offers are a long list of small problems solved, not one big promise.

**Try it on your work.** Write ten problems your customer hits on the way to the result. Turn three into solutions you could deliver cheaply. That's the start of your offer. This is P07.
