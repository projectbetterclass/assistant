**The idea.** With a good enough offer you don't need to sell hard or write great ads. Great offers are believable, say their value in a few words, and are built so the business still makes money, often by using "free" on purpose.

## Three famous offers
- Domino's: at your door in 30 minutes or it's free.
- 10 CDs for a penny (with a membership behind it).
- FedEx: when it absolutely has to be there overnight.

## What they share
1. **Believable.** A marketer once advertised "send me $1 and I'll send you $1,000" and got zero replies. Give a reason why: "we're overstocked, everything's half off."
2. **Valuable in a few words.** "30 minutes or it's free." If someone can't repeat your offer in a sentence or two, it isn't clear enough.
3. **A business behind it.** Free brings the most leads at the lowest cost, but you have to know how it pays.

## Seven ways to use "free" (his gym examples)
| Way | How it works |
|---|---|
| Free bonus | Something big free when they buy the smaller thing (all past courses free with a monthly membership) |
| Limited free | A basic free trial, then the full version with a guarantee |
| Free with a penalty | Free, but missed sessions cost money, so they actually show up |
| Free with a deposit | Put money down; get it back, or credited, if they hit the goal |
| Free forever | Give the service away and earn on something else (one clinic made about 95% of its revenue on supplements) |
| Giveaway | A prize related to your offer; everyone who enters is a lead |
| Free with commitment | First month free if they sign up for longer |

**Watch for.** It's aimed at gym owners, and several of these need careful, honest wording. Tell people clearly before you bill them: he says about 30% of trial users who get billed without being sold call to cancel.

**Try it this week.** Write your offer in one sentence a friend could repeat. Add a reason why ("I'm taking five people at a lower price to build case studies"). Pick one way to use free from the table that fits a service. Ties to P07.
