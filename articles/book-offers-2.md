**The argument.** The best offer and the best salesperson can't save you in the wrong market. Hormozi borrows the classic copywriter's line: if you had a hamburger stand, the one advantage you'd want is a starving crowd. Pick the crowd first.

## The four signs of a good market
1. **Massive pain.** They don't want it, they need it. "Nice to have" doesn't sell.
2. **Purchasing power.** They can afford to pay. Struggling students with a real problem still can't pay $2,000.
3. **Easy to target.** You can find them in one place: a group, a platform, an association, a job title.
4. **Growing.** The market is getting bigger, so you're riding a wave rather than fighting for a shrinking pie.

## The three big markets
Nearly everything people pay for falls under **health, wealth or relationships.** Start inside one, then narrow down.

## Niche down
- A general offer competes with everyone and sells cheap. The same thing aimed at one specific group can sell for many times more, because it fits them exactly.
- He walks through narrowing a broad offer step by step, with the price it can carry going up at each step.
- **Commit.** Hopping between markets resets you to zero each time. Pick one and stay long enough to get good.

**The idea worth keeping.** Check the four signs before you build anything. If one is missing, the business will feel like pushing uphill.

**Try it on your work.** Score your idea's market on the four signs: yes, not sure, no. Then narrow it once: add a job, a situation or a life stage. This is P02.
