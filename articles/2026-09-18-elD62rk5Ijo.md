**The claim.** SK hynix makes half of the high-bandwidth memory every AI chip needs. It's growing fast with record margins and trades cheaper than Micron and SanDisk. He's buying it and calls it "a great way to get rich without getting lucky."

## The business
- DRAM is 73% of revenue. That includes the HBM memory stacked next to every AI chip, plus ordinary memory that is priced like a commodity. Storage (NAND) is 27%, mostly data-center drives from the Intel unit it bought for $9B.
- Its HBM share fell from 64% to 50% in a year. Samsung shipped the newest generation first and jumped from 21% to 33%.
- Last quarter brought about $55B of revenue, up 257%, at a 76% operating margin, the highest in its history. In 2023, the last downturn, it lost about $5.9B.
- About ten customers signed fixed-price contracts of five years or more, with deposits. That makes revenue predictable, but it also missed last quarter's 30–60% price jump, and the stock fell about 10% on record earnings.
- Supply should stay tight. HBM takes three times the wafers, and a new fab takes four to five years. NVIDIA co-develops its next memory with SK hynix and is expected to take two-thirds of its newest HBM this year.

The NASDAQ share costs 40% more than the Korean one, because only 2.5% of the company can be traded in New York. If that limit rises, the NASDAQ price should fall. He says to buy the Korean shares directly if your broker allows it.

**Risks he names.** Samsung taking share, fixed contracts capping the upside, and every memory stock being priced on 75% margins. The middle of the video is an ad for GenSpark.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 18: SK hynix in Korea −9.5%, on the NASDAQ (SKHY) −9.9%, SanDisk −11.7%, Micron +1.3%. The S&P 500 is up 2.2%. Three weeks is too short to grade the call, but the gap between the two listings hasn't closed yet.
