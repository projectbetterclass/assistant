**The argument.** The last four ways are about doing: cut the big task into slices you can start now, give it big blocks of time, work with a sense of urgency, and once you've started, keep going until it's 100% complete. Then practise all 21 until eating your frog first is simply how you work.

## Slice and dice the task
- **The salami slice.** Write out every step, then do just one slice. A small piece is easier to start than the whole job, and often you'll want to do one more.
- **The urge to finish.** You have a compulsion to closure: finishing anything feels good and releases endorphins, and the bigger the task, the better it feels.
- **Swiss cheese.** Punch a hole in the task: work on it for a set time, as little as 5 or 10 minutes, then stop. Several of Tracy's friends wrote best-sellers one page, or one paragraph, a day.

## Create large chunks of time
- **Important work needs unbroken time.** Salespeople who phone prospects for one solid hour, say 10 to 11 am. Executives who set a time each day for calls. People who read 15 minutes every night, and over the years read hundreds of books.
- **Appointments with yourself.** Book 30, 60 and 90-minute blocks, and keep them as you would a meeting.
- **Make every minute count.** Work at home for two or three hours early in the morning: you'll get three times as much done as in a busy office. Plan work for flights, and use travel and waiting time, "gifts of time", for small pieces.

## Develop a sense of urgency
- **Action orientation.** High performers think, plan and set priorities, then launch fast and work steadily.
- **Flow.** Working hard on an important task can put you in flow: calm, clear and creative, with everything seeming effortless. A sense of urgency helps trigger it.
- **Momentum.** Getting started takes a lot of energy; keeping going takes much less. The faster you move, the more energy you have.
- **Do it now.** Say "do it now" to start, and "back to work" when you drift.

## Single handle every task
- **Once you start, keep going** until it's 100% done. That can cut the time it takes by 50% or more; starting and stopping can add up to 500%, because each restart means catching up and rebuilding momentum.
- **Self-discipline.** Elbert Hubbard: the ability to make yourself do what you should do, when you should do it, whether you feel like it or not. Persistence is self-discipline in action, and it builds self-respect.
- **Putting it together.** Review the 21 ways and practise them every day until they become second nature. Just do it: eat that frog.

**The idea worth keeping.** Cut it into a slice you can start now, give it a block of time, move fast, and don't stop until it's finished.

**Try it on your work.** Book a block in tomorrow's plan for your AI service's frog. Write its first slice, say "do it now" and start on time, and don't switch tasks until the block ends.
