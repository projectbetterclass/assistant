**The argument.** Ask "why am I on the payroll?" Every job comes down to a handful of results you're fully responsible for, and three core tasks that make up most of your value. Get excellent at the first and spend most of your time on the second, and you'll do more in less time, with more of it left for the people you love.

## Focus on key result areas
- **What they are.** Five to seven results you're completely responsible for: if you don't do them, nobody will. For a manager: planning, organizing, staffing, delegating, supervising, measuring and reporting. For a salesperson: prospecting, building rapport and trust, identifying needs, presenting, answering objections, closing, and getting resales and referrals.
- **Grade yourself.** Score yourself from 1 to 10 on each.
- **The weakest link.** Your weakest key result area sets the height at which you can use all your other skills. A manager who can't delegate is held back in everything else.
- **Poor performance causes procrastination.** People avoid the areas they're weak in, which makes it worse. You may be one skill away from top performance.
- **The great question.** What one skill, if I developed it and did it excellently, would have the greatest positive impact on my career? Ask your boss, colleagues and family too.

## Apply the law of three
- **Cynthia's story.** A coaching client listed 17 tasks, working 10 to 12 hours a day, six days a week. Tracy asked: if you could do only one thing on this list all day, which would add the most value? Then the second, then the third. Those three held about 90% of the value. Cynthia's boss agreed to hand off everything else: output and pay doubled within a month, on 8-to-5 days.
- **Everything else** is support work that can be delegated, outsourced or dropped.
- **The quick list.** Write your three most important goals in life in 30 seconds: the answers are as accurate as if you had three hours. In over 80% of cases they're a career or money goal, a family goal and a health goal. Grade yourself 1 to 10 on each, then repeat for career, family, money, health, growth, community, and your three biggest worries.

## Balance
- **Relationships.** About 85% of your happiness comes from relationships, and their quality depends on the time you spend face to face.
- **Quality at work, quantity at home.** Work all the time you work: start a little earlier, stay a little later, and don't waste the day, so the evenings are free.
- **Balance is ongoing.** Like a tightrope walker, you never get it once; you keep balancing.

**The idea worth keeping.** Find the five to seven results you're paid for and the three tasks that make most of your value, and spend your day on those.

**Try it on your work.** List everything you do for your AI service in a week. Circle the three tasks that add the most value, grade your key result areas from 1 to 10, and name the weakest one to work on this month.
