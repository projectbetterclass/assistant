**The idea.** Your SaaS idea comes back in year 2, once a paid service has shown you which workflow ten clients share. Each phase pays for the next, and each gate has a number you can check. Missing a gate means changing the niche or the offer, not pushing on.

## Four phases, three gates
1. **Discover, months 1–2.** 20 owner interviews, through your graduation project if you can; find one weekly task. You are here.
2. **Pilot, months 2–4.** Three firms free for 60 days, done by hand; log every edge case.
3. **Paid service, months 4–12.** €500–1,500 a month per client; automate one step at a time.
4. **Software, year 2 onward.** Turn the shared workflow into a tool for the industry; later, buy a firm.

The gates between them:
- **Same pain** (Discover to Pilot): 6 of 10 owners name the same task, with hours attached.
- **First payment** (Pilot to Paid service): a pilot client pays €300 or more a month.
- **Same workflow** (Paid service to Software): about 10 clients run on the same steps, under 1.5 hours each.

The service funds the software, and its clients' edge cases become the product. Each phase is a step on this branch, and each gate is the last piece of its step.

## The risks
The biggest risk isn't AI; it's building tools instead of talking to buyers. The second is the big software vendors adding your service as a feature. Both have clear counters.
- **You build instead of sell.** A Starter Story founder built 72 apps in 60 days and earned $0, until talking to two customers a day. *Handle it:* no new tool until the first client pays. Two conversations a day.
- **Your service becomes a feature of the client's software.** Funda sells its own AI styler; accounting packages are adding AI. *Handle it:* sell a done-for-you result, not a tool. Work inside the systems clients already use.
- **Clients do it themselves with ChatGPT.** 3 in 10 entrepreneurs already use ChatGPT or Copilot. *Handle it:* target firms with 5–50 staff and no time. Guarantee a result they can't get alone.
- **The AI gets something wrong.** Isenberg's rule for AI-run firms: a person approves before anything reaches a client. *Handle it:* a human check on every output, a corrections log, liability limits in your terms.
- **A privacy breach.** The AP reports rising breaches from staff pasting data into chatbots. *Handle it:* a data-processing agreement, business plans that don't train on data, EU hosting, a one-page AI policy per client.
- **AI costs eat the margin.** Chris Raroque's app cost 10× the estimate. *Handle it:* use the cheapest model that passes your tests; check the cost per client every month.
- **Prices fall as everyone adopts AI.** Isenberg: the gap between your costs falling and market prices catching up lasts years, not months. *Handle it:* lock in recurring clients and proof now; move towards software later.
- **Fake self-employment, or depending on one client.** Wet DBA enforcement since 2025. *Handle it:* monthly deliverables for several clients, never hours for one.
- **Too little time.** 14 minutes a weekday covers 1–2 clients. *Handle it:* part 10 — your graduation project, combined slots, a weekend block.

## Stop rules, so a bad niche doesn't eat a year
- After 20 interviews, if fewer than 6 owners name the same weekly task with hours or money attached, switch niche.
- After 60 days of offering pilots, if nobody accepts even a free pilot, change the offer, not the niche.
- After 6 months, if no client pays €300 a month or more, stop and put the slot back into your career and FIRE plan.

**Try it this week.** Write the three stop rules where you'll see them, with real dates: 20 interviews by when, 60 days of pilots ending when, and the 6-month check.
