**The claim.** Brandon interviews Phil Town (Rule #1 investing). A beaten-down stock is almost always cheap for a reason. The skill is judging how long the problem lasts. If it's one to three years, a patient small investor can buy what fund managers can't hold. Phil's team just did that with Salesforce.

## Salesforce and the "SaaSpocalypse"
- Wall Street sold software stocks on fear that AI replaces them. Phil's view: Salesforce *is* the company's record of its customers, and AI can't replace that. It can only change how Salesforce charges.
- AI should also cut Salesforce's own costs, so margins expand rather than shrink. The fund bought cheap, and one manager made millions in a day when earnings came out.
- Why Wall Street sells anyway: a fund manager who lags for a year loses the pension money, and so their job. Problems lasting longer than a year are where you have the edge.

## His method, the "4 Ms"
| M | What he checks |
|---|---|
| Meaning | Simple enough to understand, predictable numbers |
| Moat | Revenue, earnings and free cash flow growing for years |
| Management | Low debt, high return on invested capital |
| Margin of safety | A price that works even in the bad case, often half of the public value. Private companies sell for ~7× earnings, public ones ~16–17×. |

- One person owns each company. The rest of the team tries to kill the idea, Munger-style: "how would this kill me?"
- A lesson from bankruptcy court: their valuation, matching an expert who used Damodaran's methods, won the case, but lawyers' fees ate the $200M. Since then they avoid companies with much debt.
- They read the big investors' quarterly filings (13Fs) for ideas from about 60 investors they trust, then check return on capital, cash flow and debt first.

## Berkshire, Google and the market
- Berkshire added 45% to its Google stake (~$30B, its 4th-largest). Michael Burry said he's done buying Berkshire.
- Phil: "Not a Buffett purchase." Google earns about 4–5% on its price, while Apple was bought at about 11%. He thinks Greg Abel is under pressure to put ~$380B of cash to work, and that Google's $100B of data centers could need write-offs. He expects Google "back around $150 at some point."
- He wouldn't tell anyone to park money in an index fund today. The long-run P/E (Shiller CAPE) has always been followed by a crash at this level, possibly with 10–15 flat years after, like his $600 that became $400 in the 1970s.
- He worries about a debt spiral at $40T of debt and $1.2T a year of interest. Own businesses that grow in any currency.
- While waiting, they sell options to earn cash, as Buffett did while buying the Burlington Northern railroad.

**Watch for.** The interview promotes their free Sep 18–20 workshop and a paid webinar. The options returns quoted come from students who wrote in. The last ~10 minutes are about Phil's horse farm.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 4: Salesforce −11.6% (about $229), Google +3.9% (about $352, far from $150), Berkshire +1.9%, S&P 500 +1.2%. The Salesforce rebound hasn't lasted so far.
