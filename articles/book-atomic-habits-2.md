**The argument.** Lasting change starts with who you believe you are, not with what you want to get. And every habit, good or bad, runs on the same four-step loop, which gives you four places to change it.

## Chapter 2: how your habits shape your identity
- Behaviour changes at three layers: outcomes (what you get), processes (what you do) and identity (what you believe about yourself). Most people start with outcomes. Clear says start with identity.
- Two people refuse a cigarette. One says "No thanks, I'm trying to quit." The other says "No thanks, I'm not a smoker." The second has changed something deeper.
- **Every action is a vote** for the kind of person you want to become. You don't need every vote, just a majority. Small wins are the evidence that slowly changes what you believe about yourself.
- Two steps: decide who you want to be, then prove it to yourself with small wins.

## Chapter 3: how to build better habits in 4 simple steps
- A habit is a behaviour repeated often enough to become automatic. The brain learns what's worth repeating by its results.
- **The habit loop:** cue (notices a chance of reward) → craving (wants the change) → response (the habit itself) → reward (satisfies the craving, and teaches the brain to repeat it).
- That gives **the Four Laws** for building a habit: make it obvious, make it attractive, make it easy, make it satisfying. Breaking a bad habit uses the opposites: invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying. The next four notes take one law each.

**The idea worth keeping.** The identity lines on your goals ("I'm becoming someone who reads every day") aren't decoration. They're Clear's chapter 2, and every logged session is a vote for them.

**Try it this week.** Your Buddha goal still has no identity line. Write one in the app, then cast one small vote for it each day.
