**The argument.** You will never get caught up: there will always be more to do than time to do it. What sets successful people apart is that they pick their most important task, start on it, and finish it before anything else. Your "frog" is that task: the biggest, most important one, and usually the one you're most likely to put off. And you can't pick it until you're clear what you want.

## Learning from successful people
- **Tracy's start.** Few advantages, no school diploma, years of labouring jobs, then sales on straight commission. What changed it was asking successful people what they did differently, doing the same, and getting the same results. By 25, Tracy was running a 95-person sales force in six countries.
- **The simple truth.** The ability to concentrate on your most important task, do it well and finish it is the key to success. The book is 21 ways to do that, to use in any order.

## The frog
- **The saying.** If the first thing you do each morning is eat a live frog, you can go through the day knowing that's probably the worst thing that will happen to you.
- **Rule one.** If you have to eat two frogs, eat the ugliest one first: start with the biggest, hardest, most important task.
- **Rule two.** If you have to eat a live frog, it doesn't pay to sit and look at it for very long. Start without thinking about it too much.
- **The payoff.** Finishing an important task releases endorphins, a natural high. You can get positively addicted to it, and start organizing your life around finishing things.
- **The three Ds.** Decision, discipline and determination: decide to build the habit, practise until it's automatic, and keep at it until it's locked in.

## Set the table: be clear first
- **Clarity.** Vagueness about what you want, in what order and why, is a major cause of procrastination. Only about 3% of adults have clear, written goals, and they get five to ten times as much done as people with as much education and ability.
- **The seven steps.** 1. Decide exactly what you want (at work, agree it with your boss). 2. Write it down: an unwritten goal is only a wish. 3. Set a deadline, with sub-deadlines. 4. List everything you'll need to do, and keep adding. 5. Organize the list into a plan, by priority and sequence. 6. Act on the plan immediately: an average plan done well beats a brilliant plan not done. 7. Do something every single day toward your main goal.
- **The ten-goals exercise.** Write ten goals for the next year as if a year has passed and they're already true, in the present tense. Pick the one that would have the biggest positive effect on your life, and plan it, then work on it daily.

**The idea worth keeping.** Get clear on what you want, write it down, and start each day on the one task that matters most, before you can put it off.

**Try it on your work.** Write the one goal for your AI service that would make the biggest difference this year, with a deadline. Then name tomorrow's frog: the task that moves it furthest, and make it the first thing you do.
