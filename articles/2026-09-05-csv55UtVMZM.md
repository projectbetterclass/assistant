**The claim.** Wall Street assumes $7.6T of AI spending through 2031, with growth slowing to 4% a year. NVIDIA and Broadcom's numbers say it's accelerating: the five biggest cloud companies alone plan $800B this year and $1.3T next, about 30% more than Goldman's estimate for the whole world. He uses Jensen Huang's "five-layer cake" to map where the money goes, and buys the two or three leaders in each layer.

## The two earnings reports
| | NVIDIA | Broadcom |
|---|---|---|
| Quarterly revenue | $96.2B (+106%) | $29.6B (+86%) |
| AI revenue growth | +117% (data center $89B) | +221% |
| AI revenue added in a year | +$48B | +$11.5B |
| Next quarter guide (AI) | +94% | +236% |

Percentages measure speed and dollars measure size. They don't really compete: Google, Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI buy both GPUs and custom chips.

## The five layers
1. **Energy:** AI is priced per gigawatt, $25–40B per GW of NVIDIA gear. The grid adds 86 GW this year on paper, but 90% is solar, wind and batteries, so always-on power is *shrinking*, with 5+ years of waiting to connect. Owners win: **Constellation (CEG)**, **Vistra (VST)**, **GE Vernova (GEV)**.
2. **Chips:** **NVIDIA** (Vera Rubin is ramping, and this is its first ever full-year forecast, +70%) and **Broadcom** (six custom-chip customers, aiming for $115B of AI revenue next year and $230B the year after). **TSMC** makes both kinds.
3. **Infrastructure:** networking and power inside the building. He buys **Coherent (COHR)**, **Lumentum (LITE)** and **Fabrinet (FN)**, and is starting **Powell Industries (POWL)**, which has a backlog up 69% and only four analysts covering it. He also names Arista and Vertiv.
4. **Models:** the frontier labs are private. OpenAI and Anthropic have filed to go public.
5. **Applications:** the money is here. **Meta** (AI ads +27%, 12% more per ad) and **Google**.

**Risks he names.** Few: co-packaged optics could cut laser demand, though he thinks ports grow faster. The AI labs still burn cash. A sponsor segment for an AI app builder runs early on.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 4, his twelve buys average +8.6% against +1.2% for the S&P 500. Best: Lumentum +25.2%, Fabrinet +19.4%, Meta +16.5%. NVIDIA (−0.5%) and Constellation (−0.3%) are flat.

**Aswath check.** "Wall Street underestimates the market" is a claim about total spending. It doesn't say what any one company is worth. When every supplier is priced to win a bigger market, check the total: add up the revenue all their prices assume and compare it to the $1.3T that's actually being spent (L02). And growth paid for with customers' borrowed money is growth you should fade (L20).
