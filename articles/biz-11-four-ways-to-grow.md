**The idea.** Once one location or one service works, there are four ways to multiply it: open more of your own, franchise, license, or turn it into software. Each trades cash now against value later. Which fits depends on what a new location costs and how easy your service is to copy.

## The four
| Way | What it is | Fits when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your own chain | You open and run more locations | A new one costs less than about six months of a location's profit, and the work is simple to repeat | You carry every cost and every lease |
| Franchise | Others buy in and run locations under your name and system, paying you a cut of sales | Each location is expensive to open (think McDonald's) | About $750,000 to set up, slow to profit, needs 100+ locations to really pay, and 90–95% never get there |
| Licensing | You give two of name, system and fee (all three makes it a franchise, which is regulated) | You're new and need cash coming in from day one | Easy to copy; sells for little unless 80%+ of licensees stay each year |
| Software | The service becomes tech | You already have a strong software co-founder | Costly and slow; needs 80%+ of customers to stay each year to be valued as software |

- His first business, Gym Launch, was licensing: no capital needed and cash from day one.
- One of his portfolio companies switched from licensing to owning its locations and went from $0.5M to $12M a year in 14 months.
- "Don't outsource your software to a third-party team." It cost him $2–3M to learn that.

**Why it's here.** Not for now. It's a map of where a service can go, so you can pick a first service that could grow: simple, repeatable, and cheap to open again.

**Try it this week.** Nothing to do yet. Just note which of the four your service could become.
