**The claim.** People ask whether to sell, buy or wait. His answer: find where we are in the market cycle (hope → optimism → euphoria → crash) using data, not opinions. Every measure he checks says we're not at euphoria, so keep buying good companies and hold them.

## His checklist
| Test | Now | Normal / bubble |
|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 forward P/E | 20 | 10-year average 20 (17 in 2022, 23 in 2023) |
| Trailing P/E | 25 | 28 a year ago |
| This bull run | 4 years, +100% | Average 5.5 years, +265% |
| NASDAQ since ChatGPT | +150% in 4 years | +600% in the 4 years after Netscape |
| IPOs this year | ~140–150, +19% on day one | 460 in 1999, +72% on day one |
| Corrections | −19% in Apr 2025, −20% in 2022, −10% in 2023 and 2024 | 10% a year, 20% every 3 years |

- **Earnings drive it.** 87% of S&P 500 companies beat estimates (average 76%). Earnings grew 52% against 23% expected, revenue 15%, and net margin went from 15% to 17% (average 12%).
- **It's broad.** 10 of 11 sectors grew earnings. The equal-weight S&P 500 was up 16% this year against 13% for the normal one.
- **NVIDIA isn't expensive to him:** revenue +106%, and price-to-sales fell from 25 to 18.
- **The economy is fine:** 4% unemployment, 3.5% inflation, retail spending +5%. Rate cuts would add fuel.

## His plan
- Invest half your monthly budget and keep the other half aside.
- When a stock you want is 20%+ below its 52-week high, invest 150% of the budget.
- Trim 10%, 20%, 30% when a position is up 50%, 100%, 150%.
- His odds: 54% of days are up, 75% of years, 90% of decades, and every 20-year period so far.

**Watch for.** The end is a pitch for his Patreon academy ("three spots left"), his stock app and a free guide. "There is no way to lose" over 20 years is US history, not a law.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 9 the S&P 500 is up 2.3% and the NASDAQ 100 4.9%. The equal-weight index, his proof of a broad rally, is down 0.7%. The rate cuts he hoped would add fuel went the other way: the Fed raised rates on Sep 16, and the 10-year yield rose from 4.84% to 5.24%.

**Aswath check.** A P/E of 20 means an earnings yield of 5%. The 10-year Treasury now pays about 5.2%, so stocks earn barely more than a riskfree bond. A P/E is only "average" next to rates, and that's why Aswath uses the implied equity risk premium (L04, L15), not P/E history. Also, a 17% net margin against a 12% average is a peak to normalize (L08), even for the whole market.
