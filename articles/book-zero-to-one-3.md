**The argument.** A business is worth the cash it will make over its whole future. Most of a great company's value sits far ahead, so the real question isn't "will it grow this year?" but "will it still be here in ten years?" Being first matters less than being the last one standing in your market.

## What makes a monopoly last
1. **Proprietary technology:** at least ten times better than the closest substitute in some important way. Not a bit better: ten times.
2. **Network effects:** each new user makes it more useful for everyone. They have to start small; nobody joins an empty network.
3. **Economies of scale:** it gets cheaper per customer as it grows. Software does; a yoga studio barely does.
4. **Branding:** real only when there's substance behind it.

## How to build one
- **Start small and dominate.** Pick a market small enough that you can own most of it. Amazon started with books; PayPal started with eBay's power sellers.
- **Then widen in circles,** to the next closely related market.
- **Don't "disrupt".** Picking a fight with big incumbents invites them to crush you. Grow where they aren't looking.

**The idea worth keeping.** "Big market" is usually a trap for a beginner. A tiny market you can actually own beats a huge one where you're one of a thousand.

**Try it on your work.** For your best idea, write the smallest group of people you could serve better than anyone, specific enough that you could list where they gather. Which of the four would protect you there later? This is P03 and P11.
