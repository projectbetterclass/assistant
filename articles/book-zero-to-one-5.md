**The argument.** Every great business rests on a secret: something important and true that most people don't know or don't believe yet. Most people have stopped looking, because they assume everything worth discovering has been found. That makes the hunt less crowded for anyone who keeps looking.

## Why people stopped looking
- **Incrementalism:** school rewards small, correct steps, not big unknown questions.
- **Risk aversion:** a secret might be wrong, and being wrong alone feels worse than being wrong with everyone.
- **Complacency:** the comfortable have no reason to dig.
- **Flatness:** in a big connected world it's easy to think someone smarter must already have found it.

## Two kinds of secrets
- **About nature:** things not yet known about the physical world.
- **About people:** things people don't know about themselves, or hide because they're taboo or unfashionable.
- Airbnb and Uber both built on secrets that, looking back, seem obvious: spare capacity (rooms, cars) that nobody was matching to demand.

## Where to look
- Places nobody else is looking, and fields that haven't been studied closely.
- Once you find one, think hard about who to tell: usually only the people you need to make it happen.

**The idea worth keeping.** "Everyone would have thought of that" is not a reason to drop an idea. Check whether they actually have.

**Try it on your work.** Write down one thing you've seen up close (a job, a hobby, your studies) that seems broken and that people around you accept as normal. That's the raw material for the "secret" question on your idea board (P12).
