**The claim.** NVIDIA's falling margin means someone else is taking its money. NVIDIA's own footnote says who: its supply commitments jumped from $119B to $279B, "primarily memory". Brian's test for who owns a real bottleneck: compare gross margin over the last four quarters with the four before. If it rose, the company owns something scarce. If it fell while revenue boomed, it's just busy.

## Why margin, not revenue
- In a boom everyone's revenue rises. Margin only rises when you have the power to keep the extra money.
- He learned it as a buyer at Target and Amazon: with most suppliers he had leverage. With Nintendo or Xbox he took the allocation and the price he was given.
- NVIDIA's 75% margin, guided to 72–73%, is just back where it was two years ago. Its costs nearly doubled to $63B.

## The test, on his names
| Company | Gross margin change | Revenue | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| SanDisk (SNDK) | +41.4 points (26% → 85%) | nearly tripled | Scarce |
| Micron (MU) | +35.4 points (37.7% → 84.6%) | – | Scarce |
| Constellation Energy (CEG) | +24.5 points | – | Scarce: licensed reactors that already run |
| Vertiv (VRT) | +4.3 points | +26% | Passes, barely |
| GE Vernova (GEV) | +2.2 points | – | Long order book, little pricing power |
| nVent (NVT) | −2.2 points | +46% | Busy, not scarce |

- Of 24 companies in the AI chain, 13 passed, and all 24 make more gross profit than two years ago.
- Micron has signed supply deals to 2030 for about a quarter of revenue. Its CEO says key customers get only 50–67% of what they need.
- Micron trades at ~6× next year's earnings. That price only works if earnings fall ~60% below forecasts and stay there, so the market is calling a peak. Memory margins last peaked at 59% in 2018 and turned to losses within five years.
- All of them sit at the 94th–98th percentile of their own valuation history. SanDisk has no history to compare against, which he calls "a missing instrument, not a green light".

**Risks he names.** China: CXMT, a state-backed memory maker, raised $8.6B and may start ~9 wafers for every 10 Micron starts, all in ordinary memory, the product that built Micron's margin. Apple testing CXMT would give it credibility, and senators are pushing back. Memory stocks already fell 30–50% between June and August. The middle of the video is a sponsor segment for a tiny copper explorer.

**How the call aged.** Since Sep 4: Micron +1.2%, SanDisk −9.1%, Constellation −0.3%, Vertiv −13.5%, GE Vernova +6.7%. nVent, the one that failed his test, is up 7.3%. S&P 500 +1.2%. Too early to grade.

**Aswath check.** Brian's test is about the margin you'd put into a valuation, and it's exactly the problem in L08: an 85% gross margin with no precedent, against a 2018 peak of 59% followed by losses. Normalize it before you value Micron or SanDisk. Then check what the price assumes (L22). At 6× earnings, the market is already pricing in the fade.
