**The idea.** Leila calls it being a "wantrepreneur": someone dabbling in business without seeing the skills they're missing. The tell is over-consuming and over-thinking. You plan, build and polish, but never ship or sell. You don't need more information. You need imperfect action.

## What it looks like
- An idea, then building something (a product, a campaign), then never actually selling it.
- Her own example: five or six months of weekends building an online fitness course, "Queen Transformation". She never launched it.
- The root is fear of failing, dressed up as "it isn't ready yet".

## Four ways she got out
1. **Picture the worst case.** Hers: quit the job, lose her clients, run low on money, move back in with her dad at 23 and rebuild. Not that bad. What actually hurts is judging yourself for failing.
2. **Make the goal learning, not winning.** If the goal is to learn, failing still counts. Set the big goals once you're in the game.
3. **Set low expectations.** A woman six months into wholesaling made $7,000 a month and felt like a failure, because she'd expected $20,000 and compared herself to people with years of sales experience. She had been a waitress.
4. **Call it an experiment.** Six to twelve months, then look at what you learned. The worst case is going back to what you did before.

**A note for this shelf.** Reading is consuming too. Every read here ends with something to try, and a read only counts once you've done the try.

**Try it this week.** Write your worst case for starting a small service on the side, in four lines like hers. Then write the experiment: what you'll try, for how long, and what you want to learn from it.
