**The claim.** Felix still owns gold. What he's stopped buying is "gold": funds that track the price but aren't metal you control. If you want gold as insurance, buying it from the institutions you're insuring against is "a lifeboat bolted to the ship".

## The four ways in
| Way | What you really own | His verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Gold fund (GLD, $152B)** | A share of a trust that holds bars at a custodian | Fine for price exposure, wrong as insurance. He's out. |
| **The metal** (coins, bars) | Metal with no counterparty | The foundation |
| **Miners** (e.g. the GDX basket) | A business that digs gold | Leverage both ways, a smaller slice |
| **Royalty and streaming companies** | A cut of many mines' output | Miner economics with less mine risk |

## Why he left the gold fund
- **You can't get the metal.** Only a few big banks can swap shares for bars, in blocks of 100,000 shares, about $40M.
- **Your share shrinks.** The 0.40% yearly fee is paid by selling gold: one share held 0.1 oz in 2004 and about 0.09 oz now, and it keeps falling. It's in the prospectus.
- **The custody chain.** The custodian can pass bars to sub-custodians, and the fund's ability to check them is "limited".
- **Paper vs metal:** the futures exchange (COMEX) has ~14.5M oz ready for delivery (−23% in a year) against ~40M oz of open claims.

## How he'd own it
- **Metal:** buy from big dealers, since bigger pieces have smaller markups. Store it "allocated", meaning numbered bars in your name and off the storage company's books. Central banks buy bars, not fund units.
- **Miners:** costs are mostly fixed, so a 20% gold move can double profits, or wipe them out. Strikes, floods and management add risk.
- **Royalties:** they pay for a mine up front for 1–5% of revenue forever, or the right to buy ~20% of output at a fixed low price. Franco-Nevada has ~40 employees, a ~$50B market value and no debt, and keeps 70+ cents of each revenue dollar against ~40 for a miner. But a royalty on a closed mine pays nothing, and they rarely trade cheap.
- **Size:** 5–15% of a portfolio, mostly metal, with miners and royalties on top. "At 90% you've joined a cult."
