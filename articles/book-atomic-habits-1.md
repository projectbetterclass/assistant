**The argument.** Big results don't come from one big change. They come from small habits repeated until they compound. Clear rebuilt his life this way after a serious injury in high school: small, boring routines first, results years later.

## Chapter 1: the surprising power of atomic habits
- British Cycling had been mediocre for a century. In 2003 its new coach, Dave Brailsford, went after "the aggregation of marginal gains": 1% better at everything, from saddle comfort to pillows. Within about a decade British riders were winning Olympic golds and the Tour de France.
- The maths of 1%: 1% better every day for a year is about 37 times better. 1% worse every day ends near zero. Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement.
- Your current results matter less than your direction. Ask whether today's habits put you on a path you'd want in 10 years.
- **The plateau of latent potential.** An ice cube in a cold room doesn't melt as it warms from 25°F to 31°F. At 32°F it does. Early effort looks wasted, but it's stored, not lost. Most people quit in this "valley of disappointment".
- **Goals versus systems.** Goals are the results you want; systems are what you do every day. Winners and losers share the same goals, so goals can't be what separates them. A goal is a moment; a system keeps going. In his words: you don't rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems.

**The idea worth keeping.** Judge yourself by the system, not the scoreboard. For your FIRE number that means: what you invest every month matters more than where the total stands today.

**Try it this week.** Pick one goal in the app and write down the system behind it in one line: what you'll do, how often. That line, done daily, is the real goal.
