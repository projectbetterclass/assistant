**The argument.** The fourth law. We repeat what feels good *right now*. Good habits pay off later and bad ones pay off immediately, so add a small reward today.

## Chapter 15: the cardinal rule of behaviour change
- In Karachi, a researcher handed out a soap that smelled nice and lathered well. Handwashing went up, and child diarrhoea and pneumonia fell by about half. The pleasant soap made the habit satisfying.
- **The cardinal rule:** what is immediately rewarded is repeated; what is immediately punished is avoided.
- Our brains evolved for instant payoffs. Good habits need a small reward in the moment, one that fits the identity you're building.

## Chapter 16: how to stick with good habits every day
- A young stockbroker kept two jars and moved a paper clip from one to the other after every sales call. Seeing progress kept him going.
- **Habit tracking** is a reminder, a small reward and an honest record, all at once. "Don't break the chain."
- **Never miss twice.** Missing once is an accident; missing twice starts a new habit. On a bad day, do a smaller version.
- Watch out: once a number becomes the target, it stops being a good measure. Track what really matters, not just what's easy to count.

## Chapter 17: how an accountability partner can change everything
- Make a bad habit cost you something right away and in public.
- **A habit contract:** write down what you'll do and what happens if you don't, and have one or two people sign it.

**The idea worth keeping.** The seven dots on each goal and the "Missed yesterday — don't miss twice" line are chapter 16 built into your app. A filled dot is your paper clip.

**Try it this week.** Keep one goal's dots unbroken for seven days. On the hard day, do two minutes: it still counts.
