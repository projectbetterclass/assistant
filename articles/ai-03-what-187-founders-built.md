**The idea.** Most founders on Starter Story sell apps and small software, and they grew through content and social channels, not sales. LinkedIn and cold outreach, the channels that reach Dutch business owners, drove only 10 of the 187 cases.

How I counted: I sorted the 187 long case studies in your library by what they sell, read the monthly revenue from the titles (126 state one), and counted the growth channels each transcript keeps coming back to.

## What they sell
| What they sell | Cases | Median revenue in the title | Main growth channels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mobile apps | 79 | $30k a month | Influencers and creator videos, TikTok, paid ads |
| Websites and content | 33 | $40k a month | Google search, newsletters, Reddit |
| SaaS and web tools | 32 | $25k a month | Google search, Reddit, X |
| Services and agencies | 19 | $83k a month (10 state a figure) | X, newsletters, referrals |

## What the cases add to Hormozi
- **These are winners picked for a show.** One episode follows a designer who built 72 apps in 60 days and earned $0. Two customer conversations a day and daily LinkedIn posts then brought $6,500 in 60 days. [He built 100 apps and made $0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoE1yX9ogCM)
- **Building got cheap; customers didn't.** 81 of the 187 mention AI coding or no-code tools (Cursor, Claude Code, Lovable, Bubble). The scarce part is distribution.
- **Boring holds up.** Packager makes about $60k a month from one task its founder used to do by hand in an IT job. It charged $25 a month during the beta and grew through Reddit and expert partners. [Most Boring SaaS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM)
- **AI costs surprise people.** Chris Raroque's AI app cost 10× the estimate ($40 instead of $3 for 20 light users), because every tool call is a paid request. Routing simple requests to cheaper models cut it by 80%. [My AI App Cost 10x More](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-jitDTcEWio)
- **AI agencies stall; monthly packages last.** Greg Isenberg's guest calls AI automation agencies "a tar pit": one-off projects and hard sales, so most pivot within 12–18 months. A productized service with a fixed monthly deliverable is the better business. [6 Best AI Agency Niches](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6FSih5a5aIA)
- **The safe sequence: service first, software second.** Isenberg's "leveraged agency": do one boring task by hand for 3–6 months and write down every edge case. Then have AI agents follow those steps, keep the price, and aim to lift margins from about 40% to 80%. [$12.5K/mo AI Agent Business](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jEUHvkhUD_w)

**Try it this week.** Before you build anything, have two conversations with business owners. Ask what took too long last week. Write down their words, not your idea.
