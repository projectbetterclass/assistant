**The argument.** Reading doesn't get anyone interested in what you sell; advertising does. Most people do the right things but far too few times. Commit to an outcome rather than an effort, write your plan on one page, and keep rolling the die: you can't lose if you don't quit.

## The story: permission to fail
- In 2017, at a small event for eight-figure entrepreneurs, a speaker doing $3 million a month was asked which courses they took. None: they spend a set share of their ad budget on tests that may lose, and every test teaches them something.
- Hormozi tripled the ad budget the next week. Business went from $400,000 a month to $780,000, then, test by test (new audiences, text messages to leads, more and better ads, then affiliates), past $4 million a month. You either win or you learn.

## Advertising in real life: open to goal
- **The 300 flyers.** Hormozi's first flyer campaign got one call: a man whose Mercedes got scratched. A mentor tested with 5,000 flyers, then ran 5,000 a day for a month: at half a percent, 300 flyers is one and a half people.
- **Volume.** The right action in the wrong amount still fails. "100 reach-outs" means 100 a day, not 100 over six weeks.
- **Open to goal.** The Rule of 100 for the big kids: commit to an outcome, not a number of tries. A gym chain's sales managers could set their own hours as long as they signed up five members a day, whether that took until lunch or 18 hours.
- **The habit stack.** Up at 4 or 5 (which means bed early), straight to work with no routine, no meetings until noon: the longest stretch of uninterrupted work right after the longest stretch of sleep.

## The one-page advertising plan
1. **Pick the type of engaged lead.** Customers, affiliates, employees or agencies.
2. **Pick the Rule of 100 or open to goal.** Commit to your daily advertising action.
3. **Fill in the checklist.** Who (you), what (your offer or lead magnet), where (the platform), to whom (your audience or list), when (your first hours each day), why (X engaged leads), how (warm or cold outreach, content or ads), how much (100, or until you hit the goal), how many follow-ups, and for how long (100 days, or until you hit the goal).
4. **Do it daily** until you can afford to pay someone else to.
5. **Then make employees your target**, and repeat until you have the help you need.

## The roadmap, level by level
- **Level 1.** Your friends know: one offer, one avatar, one platform. Warm outreach.
- **Level 2.** Everyone you know, consistently: as much warm outreach and content as you can.
- **Level 3.** Employees advertise for you.
- **Level 4.** A product good enough for referrals: aim for 25% or more of customers from referrals before scaling again. This is where most people let the product slip.
- **Level 5.** More places, more ways, more people: at least two methods on several platforms.
- **Level 6.** Hire killers: experienced executives who run departments without you. Hire experience, not potential; if you aren't learning from them in the interview, it's the wrong person.
- **The $100M leads machine.** All of it running together, five to ten years of work. Play games where, if you wait, you win.

## The many-sided die
- You and a friend each roll a die with one green side, one with 20 sides and one with 200. Every green you roll turns another side green. You can't see how many sides you have, and the game ends when you stop.
- You keep rolling and the greens come more often. Your friend rolls a few reds, says your die must be easier, and quits. Roll enough times and the die you were given doesn't matter. The more you roll, the better you get; if you quit, you lose.

**The idea worth keeping.** Commit to the outcome, not the effort: do the obvious thing far more times than feels reasonable, and keep rolling.

**Try it on your work.** Fill in the one-page plan for your AI service: the lead type, the Rule of 100 or open to goal, and all ten lines of the checklist. Put tomorrow's first block in your calendar.
