**The argument.** Around a caravan's campfire, old Kalabab asks: would you rather have a bag of gold, or a clay tablet carved with words of wisdom? Everyone shouts "the gold!" Kalabab then tells the story of Arkad's child, Nomasir, which shows why the tablet is worth more: the gold was soon lost, and the five laws carved on the tablet won it back three times over.

## Nomasir's test
- **The gift.** Arkad gives Nomasir a bag of gold and a clay tablet with the five laws, and sends them away for ten years. Come back and prove yourself, and you'll inherit the estate; otherwise it goes to the priests.
- **Losing the gold.** In a caravan to Nineveh, two friendly travellers with a fast white horse talked Nomasir into betting against a rich man's unbeaten horse. Nomasir's horse was badly beaten: the rich man was their partner. Then a young companion proposed a shop partnership, let Nomasir buy the stock, and spent foolishly until nothing was left.
- **Starting over.** With no trade, Nomasir sold the horses, the slave and the spare robes, then read the tablet properly. Work managing a crew building Nineveh's new outer wall came next, and Nomasir saved a tenth of it.
- **Winning it back.** The crew's master, impressed by the saving, invited Nomasir into a group pooling gold to fetch copper and tin for the city's bronze gates. It paid well, and Nomasir stayed with that group of careful investors.
- **Coming home.** After ten years, Nomasir placed three bags of gold before Arkad: one for the bag of gold, two for the tablet of wisdom.

## The five laws
1. **Gold comes gladly**, and in increasing amounts, to anyone who puts aside at least a tenth of their earnings to build an estate for themselves and their family.
2. **Gold works diligently** and contentedly for the wise owner who finds it profitable work, multiplying like the flocks of the field.
3. **Gold clings to the cautious owner** who invests it on the advice of people wise in handling it.
4. **Gold slips away** from anyone who invests it in a business or purpose they don't know, or that people skilled in it wouldn't approve.
5. **Gold flees** from anyone who tries to force impossible earnings from it, follows the tempting advice of tricksters and schemers, or trusts their own inexperience and romantic dreams.

**The idea worth keeping.** Save a tenth, put it to work, take advice from people who know, invest only in what you understand, and walk away from promises of impossible returns.

**Try it on your work.** Check one investment in your FIRE plan against laws three to five: who advised it, could you explain how it earns money, and is the return it promises realistic?
