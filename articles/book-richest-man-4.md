**The argument.** Around a caravan's campfire, Kalabab asks: would you rather have a bag of gold, or a clay tablet carved with words of wisdom? Everyone picks the gold. Kalabab then tells the story of Arkad's child, Nomasir, which shows why the tablet is worth more: the gold was soon lost, and the five laws carved on the tablet won it back many times over.

## Nomasir's test
- **The gift.** Arkad gives Nomasir a bag of gold and a clay tablet with the five laws, and sends them away for ten years to prove they can handle money.
- **Losing the gold.** Nomasir loses the gold to tricksters at the horse races, and then to a partnership in a business neither partner understood.
- **Winning it back.** Starting over as a hired worker, Nomasir studies the tablet, saves a tenth, invests only on good advice, and comes home after ten years with more than Arkad gave.

## The five laws
1. **Gold comes gladly** to anyone who puts aside at least a tenth of their earnings for the future.
2. **Gold works diligently** for the wise owner who finds it profitable work, and multiplies like flocks in the field.
3. **Gold clings to the cautious owner** who invests it on the advice of people wise in handling it.
4. **Gold slips away** from anyone who invests in a business or purpose they don't understand, or that people skilled in it wouldn't approve.
5. **Gold flees** from anyone who tries to force impossible returns from it, follows the tempting advice of tricksters and schemers, or trusts their own inexperience and wishful thinking.

## Why the tablet beats the gold
- **Gold without the laws** is soon lost; the laws without gold soon earn it.
- **Most losses break laws four and five:** investing in what you don't understand, and chasing returns that are too good to be true.

**The idea worth keeping.** Save a tenth, put it to work, take advice from people who know, invest only in what you understand, and walk away from promises of impossible returns.

**Try it on your work.** Check one investment in your FIRE plan against laws three to five: who advised it, could you explain how it earns money, and is the return it promises realistic?
