**The argument.** In the Temple of Learning, a group of Babylonians sets out to find how to attract good luck. Gambling isn't it: the people who run the games always win in the end. The luck worth having comes through work and trade, as opportunities, and what loses them is putting off the decision.

## Where luck doesn't come from
- **The gaming tables.** One student hoped good luck meant winning at dice or the races. Arkad points out that the games are set up so the people who run them profit; nobody can count on luck there.
- **Where it does.** Good luck shows up as opportunities in your own work and trade, and goes to the people who take them.

## The missed opportunities
- **The land outside the walls.** One student had been invited, as a young person, to join neighbours in buying cheap dry land and irrigating it, paying a tenth of their earnings each month. They kept meaning to join, and never did. The others became rich from it.
- **The flock of sheep.** A trader was offered a whole flock at a bargain price one evening, after the city gates had closed. They decided to wait until morning; by then, others had bought it at the bargain price and sold it on for a big profit.
- **The common thread.** Both knew a good opportunity when they saw it, and both put off deciding.

## Procrastination is the enemy
- **Why we hesitate.** Fear, comfort, and a habit of waiting until we're sure. By then, the opportunity has usually gone.
- **People of action.** The goddess of good luck favours people who act when the opportunity is in front of them.

**The idea worth keeping.** Luck is an opportunity taken; the habit that loses it is putting off the decision.

**Try it on your work.** Name one opportunity for your AI service you've been meaning to decide on: a client to approach, an offer to test, a price to set. Decide it today, and take the first step.
