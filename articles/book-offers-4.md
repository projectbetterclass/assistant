**The argument.** Value isn't a feeling. Hormozi breaks it into four parts. Two you push up, two you push down, and an offer is worth more the further you move each one.

## The equation
**Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of success) ÷ (Time to get there × Effort and sacrifice)**

| Lever | Direction | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Up | What they really want, in their words (not "fitness" but "fit into my wedding dress") |
| Perceived likelihood | Up | How sure they are it'll work for them: proof, reviews, guarantees, your track record |
| Time delay | Down | How soon they see a result, including a quick first win |
| Effort and sacrifice | Down | How little they have to do or give up |

## What it shows
- Most businesses only push the dream outcome ("lose 20 kg!"). The bottom half is where the cheap, big wins are.
- **Making it faster and easier often costs you little and adds a lot of value.** Done-for-you is valuable mainly because it drives effort to almost zero.
- **A quick first win** (a result in the first week) increases both perceived likelihood and how fast it feels.
- The same core service can be worth very different prices depending on these four.

**The idea worth keeping.** If you want to charge more, look at the bottom of the equation first: faster and easier.

**Try it on your work.** For your service, write one change for each of the four levers. Circle the one that costs you least. This is P07.
