**The argument.** As leads grow, so does the work, and one person can only do so much. Employees can do the Core Four for you once you train them, and a business that gets customers without you is worth far more. Agencies are worth paying, but to learn a new method or platform from, not to rely on forever.

## The story: cold outreach that kept missing its goal
- Two quarters in a row the cold outreach team missed. Nobody asked why. The answer was hiring: a rep left every four weeks, only one in four candidates was hired, and it took 40 one-to-one interviews to find each one.
- Hormozi switched to group interviews, screening out only the "crazies" and teaching everyone with a work ethic and basic social skills. Within six weeks hiring beat churn, and cold outreach sales doubled to over half of all sales. The method was fine; there just weren't enough people doing it.

## Employees
- **Trade doing for managing.** Swap 40 hours of doing for 4 of managing, then 200 hours of work for 20 of managing, then hand that to a manager who takes 4 of yours.
- **Why it makes you wealthy.** A $2 million-profit business that needs you round the clock is a high-paying job. The same business running without you could be worth $10 million. You get rich from what you make, wealthy from what you own.
- **Everyone is replaceable, especially me.** "If you want it done right, do it yourself" is false: get someone to spend all their time doing it.
- **The internal Core Four.** You find employees the same way as customers: ask your network (warm outreach), recruit (cold outreach), post job openings (content), promote the postings (ads); plus employee referrals, staffing firms, and associations and guilds.
- **The 3 Ds: document, demonstrate, duplicate.** Write the checklist and check you get an A+ following only it; do it in front of them; then they do it in front of you, and you fix the checklist until the result matches. Could a stranger get your results from the checklist alone?
- **Training notes.** If they get it wrong, the checklist is wrong. Slow, then smooth, then fast. Praise following the directions, one piece of feedback at a time, no punishment. Entry-level jobs take more grit than skill: properly trained and still below the bar in three weeks, let them go.
- **The numbers.** Payroll divided by engaged leads is your cost per lead. Acquisition.com gets about 30,000 leads a month from a $100,000-a-month content team: $3.33 a lead.
- **Which problem?** Do your engaged leads have the problem you solve and the money to spend? If not, it's an advertising problem; if they do and still don't buy, it's a sales problem. Don't fire the wrong team.

## Agencies
- **The story: "my time's not for sale".** In 2016 Hormozi asked an ad agency owner to teach rather than run the Facebook ads. The answer was $750 an hour. Eight weekly sessions, with homework and recordings, and about $6,000 later, the skill was learned for good.
- **The cycle of insanity.** The agency gets you excited, their best rep starts on your account, you see results, the rep moves to newer clients, a junior takes over, results slide, you cancel and find another agency.
- **Use them to learn.** "I want to do what you do in my business. I'd like to work with you for six months, I'll pay extra for you to explain why you make each decision, then I'll train my team and switch to a cheaper consulting arrangement." For YouTube: one agency to get going, a pricier elite one to learn to maximize it. Once your team beats them, drop to consulting.
- **Picking a good one.** Someone you know got results; recognisable clients; a waiting list; realistic expectations; long-term strategy, not hacks; clear about what they need from you; regular, simple updates and tracking; a good offer. Good ones are expensive, but not every expensive one is good. Talk to five or ten first.

**The idea worth keeping.** Other people can do what you do if you write it down and train them. Pay agencies to teach you, not to hold your business by the throat.

**Try it on your work.** Pick the lead-getting task you do most for your AI service. Write its checklist so precisely that a stranger could follow it, then do it once following only the checklist.
