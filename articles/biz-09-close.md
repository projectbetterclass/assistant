**The idea.** When you're selling, only three things should come out of your mouth: questions, their own words said back to them, and short stories that show what working with you would be like. Teaching tactics doesn't sell and barely helps. Breaking the belief that keeps them stuck does both.

## The three things
1. **Questions, mostly.** Why are you here? What are you struggling with? Where are you trying to get to? What have you tried, and how did it go? Why does this matter to you? People only change their mind when they reach the conclusion themselves.
2. **Say it back.** "So what I'm hearing is: this is the main problem, this is what you've tried, and this is why it hasn't worked. Does that sound right?"
3. **Short stories.** Instead of explaining your method (he calls it technobabble), a quick analogy that breaks the belief. His weight-loss one: you won't give up cake for life, so we find food you like more than what you eat now, and then you'll stick to it.

## Why not teach
- Early on he gave gym owners advice on the call. They took notes, didn't buy, and didn't use the advice either.
- A friend's line: everyone benefits from breaking a belief; only about 10% benefit from tactics.

## His rules
- Don't pitch until you believe they'll say yes. Like a proposal, you ask when you already know the answer.
- If they ask "how does it work?" too early, say you need to understand more first.
- You can usually tell in the first five to ten minutes. If it's going badly, ask a harder question that challenges their view, and let them argue that there is a way.
- The real secret is to actually care. A script only copies how someone who cares would talk.

**Try it this week.** Write the questions above for your service, in your own words, plus one short story for the most common reason people don't start. Ties to P04: the Mom Test also runs on questions about their life, not your idea.
